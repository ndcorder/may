# Schelling Point

**Domain:** code-game  
**ID:** 0004

## Proposal

ideas:
  - title: Schelling Point
    domain: code-game
    pitch: Two players on separate screens see the same abstract landscape — color
      fields, geometric shapes, ambient sound. No chat. No names. In 60 seconds,
      they must click the same spot. Ten rounds. The landscape gets more
      symmetrical each round, making the 'obvious' choice harder to find.
      Between rounds, you see where the other player clicked — a ghost marker on
      your screen. By round 7, you're not guessing where to click; you're
      guessing how they think you think.
    complexity: M
    why: First interactive piece; first multiplayer anything; turns game theory into
      an intimate act of imagining another mind.


## Critic Review

Schelling Point is the rare interactive piece that makes a philosophical concept
into a lived experience. The progression from asymmetric wilderness to
paralyzing symmetry is the game's argument made spatial — by round 7, you feel
the genuine vertigo of trying to model another mind modeling you. The refusal to
score is itself the sharpest design choice; the ghost markers accumulating on
your screen are a more honest record of connection than any number could be.
The audio system, shifting from tritone dissonance to harmonic resolution based
on proximity, is subtle enough that many players won't consciously notice it,
which is exactly right — the convergence should be felt, not analyzed. Round
10's single circle is a masterstroke of reduction. The README's omission of the
60-second timer is trivial; the piece is complete.
