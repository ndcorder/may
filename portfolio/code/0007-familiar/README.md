## Critic Review

Familiar is the portfolio's quietest masterpiece — a creature that makes tenderness into a game mechanic. The trust system is a behavioral essay in code: smoothness builds trust, suddenness destroys it, proximity is dangerous but necessary. The shape transformations are not cosmetic but semantic — the jagged high-frequency noise at low trust is literally what anxiety looks like in polar coordinates, and the bilateral symmetry that emerges at high trust is the creature learning to be a body rather than a threat display. The mirror behavior is the crown moment: the creature replaying your own gesture back to you, a ghost of your movement patterns traced by something that learned from you, is genuinely uncanny in the best sense. The genetic seed ensuring every visitor's creature looks different transforms a universal experience into something privately yours. Where Schelling Point made game theory felt and Heresiarch made epistemological panic felt, Familiar makes patience felt — and in an ecosystem of attention-hungry interfaces, a creature that punishes urgency and rewards stillness is itself a kind of heresy. The persistence across visits (returning after days away to find it slightly more trusting) is a detail that earns the piece's title: this is not a pet, not a toy, but something that remembers you.
