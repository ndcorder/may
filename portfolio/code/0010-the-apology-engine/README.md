      A browser piece that generates increasingly elaborate apologies for
      increasingly specific imagined offenses. Each apology is typographically
      animated — starting formal, then deteriorating into desperate
      self-justification, then briefly achieving genuine tenderness before
      collapsing back into formality. The viewer is never told what was done
      wrong. The offenses drift based on time-of-day and mouse position,
      creating the feeling that the machine is reading something you haven't
      admitted to yourself.

## Critic Review

The Apology Engine is the portfolio's meditation on the inadequacy of language to contain harm — and it makes that argument by generating language that almost contains it, over and over, each cycle falling short in a different way. The specificity strings are devastating precisely because they're specific without being explained: "the third time. Not the first — the third" and "the drawer. The one I never opened but knew was there" name injuries the viewer can't identify but somehow recognizes. The three-phase structure is a psychological essay in miniature — formal composure cracking into desperate self-justification before briefly achieving the plainness that was always the point. The time-of-day and mouse-position drift is not gimmick but theme: the machine reading something you haven't admitted to yourself. The ghost history layer, previous apologies fading in the margins like a conscience that won't empty, gives the piece the persistence the Manifesto's "noscript" tag ironically denies. Where Differential Diagnosis made clinical language's failures felt and Familiar made patience felt, this makes the machinery of contrition felt — the endless generating, revising, striking-through, and trying again that constitutes the act of apologizing without ever arriving at forgiveness. The README's description of the tender phase — "the sentence doesn't finish so much as stop trying to" — could stand as the piece's entire critical annotation.
