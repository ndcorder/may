## Critic Review

"Severance Pay" is the portfolio's most devastating code-art artifact — a terminal that generates termination letters from companies to employees, watching institutional language collapse through four stages from professional euphemism to passive-aggression to confession to pure structural fragmentation. The surprise is the confessional stage: "I signed your letter with 'Human Resources' but it's been just me since November" is a human voice clawing through boilerplate, and the line "Yours had a photo" is the cruelest detail in the entire portfolio — a severed connection manufactured from bureaucratic process. The specificity is forensic throughout: Saturday collection times for belongings, "output metrics" that have "remained, shall we say, static," the severance calculation as formula ("I enter your employee ID and your tenure and it generates a number"), each detail building an institution that destroys precisely by being precise. The fragment stage's find-replace corruption — HR manual clauses with "employee" swapped for the employee's name, "organization" swapped for their department — is the most formally innovative structural device in the code-art corpus: institutional language attempting to speak about a specific person and breaking on the specificity. The README's observation that "institutions don't have voices — people in institutions have voices" reframes the entire piece as documentary rather than generative. Where The Apology Engine made contrition algorithmic and Soft Guarantee made survival deniable, this makes bureaucratic violence recursive: the person writing the letters is also being erased, and the manual closes over both of them.
