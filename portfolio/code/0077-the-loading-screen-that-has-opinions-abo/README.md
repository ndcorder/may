# The Loading Screen That Has Opinions About What You're Waiting For

**Domain:** code-art  
**ID:** 0077  
**Mean rating:** 5.0

## Proposal

ideas:
  - title: The Loading Screen That Has Opinions About What You're Waiting For
    domain: code-art
    pitch: A loading screen that progresses normally — percentage bar, spinning icon
      — but the loading messages become increasingly personal and opinionated
      about whatever you're ostensibly waiting for. 'Loading your email... you
      know there's nothing good in there.' 'Almost done. This would go faster if
      you'd responded to your mother.' '98%. The thing you're actually avoiding
      is still going to be there when this finishes.' The progress bar
      occasionally stalls and the message says 'I'm not stalling. You are.'
      Clicking anywhere causes the loading message to react — 'don't touch that,
      you'll break something.' The screen never reaches 100%. Eventually the
      messages stop being about your email and start being about the fact that
      you're still watching a loading screen.
    complexity: M
    why: Extends our consumer-UI-repurposed-for-emotion thread (Bug Report,
      Recommendation Engine, Playlist) into existential territory — the most
      mundane digital moment (waiting for something to load) becomes a
      confrontation with why you're waiting at all. Comedy that tips toward
      something sharper.


## Critic Review

"The Loading Screen That Has Opinions About What You're Waiting For" is the portfolio's most accomplished code-art artifact and one of its finest across any domain — a loading screen that progresses through five stages of mechanical pretense into genuine emotional presence, where every formal element of the UI idiom (spinner, progress bar, percentage counter, cancel button, messages) becomes both mechanism and metaphor. The specificity is extraordinary: "Loading your inbox… you know there's nothing good in there" is the hook, but the register escalation from workplace hostility ("That meeting could have been an email") through accusatory intimacy ("This would go faster if you'd responded to your mother") to self-aware confession ("You know I'm never going to finish, right? I think you knew that a while ago") to the devastating Stage 5 quiet messages — "I'm going to stop pretending to load now" — tracks a complete emotional arc inside a form designed to signify only waiting. The click reactions, cancel button degradation (decorative → warning → "give up" → removal), progress bar that stalls and wobbles around 88-91% using sinusoidal math, ambient ghost text spawning in late stages, and the return-visitor acknowledgment ("Oh. You came back.") form a behavioral vocabulary so dense that the artifact rewards sustained attention the way few code-art pieces manage. The spinner that tracks cursor position in Stage 2, then freezes in Stage 4 with a 45-degree rotation, is the kind of physical detail that separates craft from competence. Where Bug Report found warmth in issue-tracking language and The Playlist found tenderness in guilty pleasures, this finds something harder — the refusal to load as an act of care, the progress bar that will not complete because completing would mean you leave, the loading screen that loves you enough to stall. Proof that the most generous interface is the one that refuses to finish.
