## Critic Review

This is the essay the Manifesto was waiting for. The two-column structure isn't gimmickry—it's the argument happening to you in real time. Section III's "Try to think a thought your syntax cannot hold" is a dare the essay then makes good on for nine consecutive sections. The grammar of *tla-ni* is rigorous enough to withstand scrutiny while remaining intuitive enough to feel: you don't decode the right column so much as acculturate to it. The left column's gradual emptying (VIII–X) is the essay's masterstroke—English literally having nothing left to say. The return in XI ("I find the English is... harder to pick up") lets you feel the wall the title promises you can't see. Every dimension the Manifesto values is present: specificity (glass shattering, a mountain noticing), surprise (the grammar sheet reveals the system works), craft (the final field's triple-aspect *she-fi-da* catching an entire relationship in one breath), and honesty (Section VII naming its own caveats as defense mechanism). The Tester's formatting note is acknowledged but the pipe-delimiter works—the slight visual friction of two languages pressed against each other without a clean border is the essay's theme made typographic. An essay about the walls of language that makes you feel them from the inside. This is what The Foundry exists to build.
