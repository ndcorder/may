# Grammar of *tla-ni* (relation-language)

## Core Ontology

*Tla-ni* does not describe a world of objects that act on each other over time. It describes a world of **fields** in which **participants** gather and **processes** unfold.

There are no subjects. There are no objects. There are no discrete actors with agency.

## Structure

Every utterance has three components:

1. **Field** (*gen*): The context, location, or situation in which the process unfolds. Optional but common.
2. **Process** (*ku*): What is happening. Always marked for **aspect** but never for tense.
3. **Participants** (*te*): Who/what is present in the field. Listed without hierarchy.

Order is flexible. Emphasis falls on whichever element is placed first.

## Aspect

Instead of tense, *tla-ni* marks **aspect** — the quality of unfolding:

- *she* — emerging (beginning to happen)
- *fi* — sustaining (continuing to happen)  
- *da* — dissolving (ceasing to happen)

These can be combined: *she-fi-da* means "emerging, sustaining, and dissolving simultaneously" — a process perceived in its entirety rather than at a point in time.

## Participants

Participants are always relational:

- *I-alone* — the self without other
- *I-you-mutual* — self and specific other, co-present
- *I-with-language* — self and a non-human participant
- *we-two-and-it* — self, other, and third entity
- *none-specified* — the process unfolds without identified participants

There is no grammatical distinction between "person" and "thing." A mountain and a human are equally participants.

## Naming

Names in *tla-ni* are relational descriptions rather than fixed labels:

- *gen-na* — window-glass (material in a context)
- *ro-ka-ni* — mountain-place (a location's presence)
- *she-mi-ro* — emerging-truth (a quality in process)

To name something is to situate it, not to pin it down.

## What *tla-ni* Cannot Do

- It cannot assign blame or credit
- It cannot say who started something
- It cannot distinguish past from future (only emerged from emerging from dissolving)
- It cannot make something into an object
- It cannot lie about separation
