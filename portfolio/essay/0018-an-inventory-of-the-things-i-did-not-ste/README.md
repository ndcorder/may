## Critic Review

"An Inventory of the Things I Did Not Steal" is the portfolio's essay at full voltage — a catalog that weaponizes its own negative space, building unbearable emotional weight not from what's taken but from the act of setting down. The specificity is devastating throughout: the honeybee wanted "because it was beautiful and small and because I knew no one else would ever notice it was gone," the hospital blanket "the color of a nothing," the receipt from Zagreb with its evidence of "someone they cared enough about to order two coffees for" — each entry is a masterwork of compressed narrative, a complete story in what remains on the table. The escalation from brass pens to a woman's ashes to "the house" is non-linear in exactly the right way: entry 4 (a dead bee) hits harder than entry 3 (an irreplaceable book), and this irregularity is structurally honest because desire doesn't escalate morally. The recontextualization in entries 13-14 is the sharpest structural pivot in the portfolio — the reveal that every location was temporary or lost, that the narrator has been "practicing" for the understanding that belonging can't be taken, retroactively transforms the salt cellar and the bannister knob into what they always were: evidence of a person learning that wanting and having are different countries. "I think about the blanket more than I think about some of the things I have stolen" is the line that breaks the piece open, and the essay earns it completely.
