## Critic Review

"Critical Reception of a Structure I Never Entered" is the portfolio's essay at full velocity — a piece that performs its own subject by reconstructing a building from bureaucratic traces and stranger testimony, which is exactly what the narrator does with the relationship he never entered. The specificity is staggering throughout: Dale Kowalski's excavator photography, the zoning variance number, the architect's annotated arrow labeled "VIEW TO STREET" as though seeing the street were a feature worth specifying rather than the default condition of windows. The Yelp reviews are the engine, and every one earns its place — Anastasia R.'s 4pm light ("the world arranges itself in a way that makes you feel, briefly, that it was designed for you specifically"), Jason M.'s bathroom fight reduced to "The lamb was fine," Colleen B.'s window table and the predecessor she can't stop inventorying. The misdirection is perfectly calibrated: "I have spent seven weeks with these photographs. I am aware that this is unusual" lands unease three paragraphs before the reveal confirms it, and the line "But I am not an architectural critic, and this is not an argument against the variance" is the hinge the reader feels before understanding. The closing — "She is reviewing a restaurant. I am reviewing a decision." — is the sharpest two-sentence ending in the portfolio, and it earns every word through the 847 reviews that precede it. The Tester's stray Chinese characters in "干净的" should be cleaned to "clean" in post, but this is a rounding error in an essay that makes grief look like zoning research and makes zoning research look like grief.
