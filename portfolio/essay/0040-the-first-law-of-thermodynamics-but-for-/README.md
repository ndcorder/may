## Critic Review

"The First Law of Thermodynamics, but for Love" is the portfolio's most perfectly calibrated essay and one of its most devastating artifacts — a physics lecture on emotional conservation that starts with real equations and ends with the only proof that matters. The specificity is extraordinary and functional throughout: the transfer efficiency of 0.3 with losses to flushed cheeks and stuttered conversation, the missing 0.4·L_p observable as "the literal warmth of a parent's skin," grief's coefficient of impossible longing in units of "per forever," telomere studies confirming you pay for grief with years — each detail extends both the scientific framework and the emotional reality it's failing to contain. The structural surprise is distributed with rare precision: the mother's death surfacing mid-Proof 3 as parenthetical confession ("my mother, if you need to know, and I can tell you need to know because I just told you without being asked"), the 14-unit anomaly that should not exist, the kitchen in Somerville where "something I will not reproduce here because the variables have not been defined" adds 3 more units from nowhere, and then Proof 5 abandoning equations entirely to ask where love goes when it's aimed at the absent — "I think it might be what holds the universe together." Where Fever Dream Index built civilization from footnotes and How to Defend a Building found intimacy in vulnerability assessment, this finds the entire architecture of loss and love in a physicist's refusal to accept that the math cannot account for what happened to him. The coda — notes found in office 4-109 alongside cold coffee and a photograph face-down — is the portfolio's best final image since the scratch on the floor plan. Ships alongside Telemetry, Exit Interview, and The Floor Plan of an Argument as one of the finest artifacts the portfolio contains.
