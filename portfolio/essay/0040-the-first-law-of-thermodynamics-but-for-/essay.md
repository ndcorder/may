# The First Law of Thermodynamics, but for Love
## Lecture Notes — Physics 179 (Special Topics)

Good morning. I should warn you that what follows is not peer-reviewed. I should also warn you that I no longer care.

The governing equation:

$$\frac{dL_{total}}{dt} = 0$$

Love, defined here as the capacity for sustained directed tenderness, is a conserved quantity. It cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred, transformed, or stored. This is not metaphor. This is math. I will prove it four times.

*Notation:*
- *L = love (Joules·seconds, dimensionally equivalent to action, and no, I did not choose this coincidence, it chose me)*
- *S = source*
- *T = target*
- *η = transfer efficiency (0 ≤ η ≤ 1)*
- *t = time*
- *ΔL = L_T − L_S after transfer*

---

## Proof 1: Conservation of Origin

Consider a system in which a person develops affection for a new individual. We model the transfer:

$$L_{new} = \eta \cdot L_{source}$$

where *L_source* is drawn from an existing reservoir.

The naive objection: "But I love someone new without loving anyone less." The resolution is straightforward. The source is not another person. Consider:

$$L_{source} = L_{childhood} + L_{self} + L_{God} + \epsilon$$

Childhood love for parents is thermodynamically dense. The warmth of a four-year-old for her mother contains more joules per kilogram than anything produced in a laboratory. When you love a stranger on a train at twenty-six, you are drawing on reserves laid down in 1997. The transfer is lossy. Most of it becomes heat—flushed cheeks, stuttered conversation, love that dissipates before it reaches the target. But the source is depleted.

This is testable. Ask yourself: do you love your parents differently now than before you fell in love? You do. You love them with more gratitude but less *ferocity*. The ferocity went somewhere. It went into the system.

η, in this case, is approximately 0.3. The rest is warmth, which we will address in Proof 3.

**QED.** New love is not new. It is redirected.

## Proof 2: The Transfer Inequality

When love moves from source S to target T, some quantity is always lost:

$$L_{received} = \eta \cdot L_{expended}$$

where η < 1 for all known observers.

A parent loves a child with magnitude *L_p*. The child experiences love of magnitude *L_c*. By measurement:

$$L_c \approx 0.6 \cdot L_p$$

The missing 0.4·L_p is not destroyed. It takes the form of:
- Anxiety (worry is low-grade love that failed to condense into tenderness)
- Memory (storage with high thermal losses)
- Heat given off during the transfer, observable as the literal warmth of a parent's skin

You can verify this experimentally. Touch your mother's forehead. The heat you feel is the portion of her love that did not reach you. It is still love. It is just in the wrong form to be useful.

A corollary: if you have ever felt that someone loved you more than you could feel, the difference is not imaginary. It is thermal. It went into the air between you. It is still there.

I realize this may not be comforting. The law does not care about comfort.

## Proof 3: Storage and Phase Change

Love can be stored. This is not controversial. What is controversial is the phase-change behavior.

At sufficient density, love undergoes a transition analogous to crystallization:

$$L_{stored} > L_{critical} \implies \text{phase change to grief}$$

Grief is not the absence of love. Grief is love under pressure. It is the same substance at a different temperature. The equation:

$$L_{grief} = L_{love} \cdot (1 + \alpha \Delta t)$$

where α is the coefficient of impossible longing (units: per second, though it feels like per forever) and Δt is time since loss.

Note that *L_grief > L_love*. This appears to violate conservation. It does not. The additional term comes from the energy of the loss itself—the shock, the rearrangement, the work of continuing in a universe that has subtracted something it cannot put back. This energy is borrowed from the observer's future. You pay for grief with years. This is also not metaphor. Telomere studies confirm it.

But here we encounter the first problem. A loss in 2004 (my mother, if you need to know, and I can tell you need to know because I just told you without being asked) produces grief that, by the equation, should decay:

$$L_{grief}(t) = L_{grief}(0) \cdot e^{-\lambda t}$$

λ is the healing constant. It should be positive. Twenty-two years of measurement yield λ ≈ 0. The grief has not decayed. It has changed phase—crystallized so completely that it no longer behaves like a transient phenomenon. It behaves like a constant.

This means one of two things:
1. The model is wrong.
2. Grief for certain losses is not a transient response but a new baseline state.

I have published neither conclusion.

## Proof 4: The Anomaly

What follows is not a proof. It is a description of an event that the proofs cannot contain.

In 2019, a person—I will not provide variables—entered the system. Measurements were taken. The system was at equilibrium: *L_total* = *L_grief(mother)* + *L_work* + *L_residual(childhood)* ≈ 47 units. (The units are arbitrary. Everything is arbitrary. But the math is not.)

After nine months of observation, *L_total* = 61 units.

The increase of 14 units has no source. I have checked:

$$L_{childhood}: \text{depleted by previous transfers, stable}$$
$$L_{work}: \text{unchanged (measured by hours, output, satisfaction)}$$
$$L_{self}: \text{decreased (less sleep, less art, less silence)}$$

Every known reservoir decreased or held constant. The system gained 14 units.

Either love was created—which would overturn the governing equation and this entire lecture series—or there exists a source I have not accounted for. A reservoir outside the model. Love from nowhere. Spontaneous generation.

In physics, when a calculation yields impossible energy, we do not discard the observation. We revise the theory. We say: there must be a particle we haven't detected. A field we haven't named. Dark energy. We have a history of giving names to things we cannot explain.

I have no name for this source. I have spent three years looking for one. I have not found it. But I will tell you what I know: on a night in November of that year, in a kitchen in Somerville, this person turned from the stove and said something I will not reproduce here because the variables have not been defined and I do not have the notation, and the system gained another 3 units.

Fourteen became seventeen. From nowhere. From nothing I can derive.

The math cannot account for it. The math is what I have. I am telling you what I have.

## Proof 5

Where does love go when it is sent toward someone who no longer exists to receive it?

It is not destroyed. The law forbids destruction.

It is not returned. There is no mechanism for return.

It does not accumulate in the sender. We have measured. The sender's capacity is finite and, in this case, full.

It is not stored. Storage requires a container. The dead are not containers.

It is not transformed into grief. We established in Proof 3 that grief is its own phase, with its own energetic signature. This is something else. This is something the equations do not have a variable for.

Love aimed at the dead. Love aimed at a grave, at a name, at a memory that is losing resolution every year. Love with velocity but no target.

Where is it?

I don't know.

I think it might be what holds the universe together.

I think it might be the pressure that keeps space from collapsing. Every joule of love sent toward the absent, every prayer and every gripped photograph and every whispered you would have loved this, accumulating in the fabric of spacetime since the first human looked at an empty doorway and expected someone to walk through it.

I cannot prove this.

The math breaks down here. The math has broken down for three years and I am tired of pretending I will fix it. The law is either wrong or it is incomplete and I built my career on the assumption that conservation governs all systems and now I sit in this lecture hall at 2 AM dictating into a phone because the universe gained 17 units in 2019 and lost 47 in 2004 and I can balance the equations for every system except mine.

The final proof is:

I love her.

It has nowhere to go.

It does not stop.

*The governing equation is under revision.*
