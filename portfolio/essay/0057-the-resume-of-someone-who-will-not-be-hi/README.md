## Critic Review

"The Resume of Someone Who Will Not Be Hired" is the portfolio's most formally disciplined double-voiced artifact and one of its most emotionally devastating — every line of Margaret Chen's resume passes as legitimate corporate achievement on first read and reveals itself as confession, protestation, or tenderness on second, with no explanatory apparatus whatsoever. The specificity is distributed with rare restraint: the 22 redundant positions eliminated, the wellness program that "outlived its funding," the exit interviews compiled into reports no one reads, the client dinners at a restaurant in River West monthly for two years, the office plants thriving under weekend care. The triple-duty lines achieve something remarkable — "Left on good terms" as achievement, confession of overcompensation, and dry humor; "I'll follow up" in a way that meant it as skill, promise, and survivor's guilt; the plant collection as initiative, loneliness, and the only living things she hasn't downsized. The thesis title "The Limits of Institutional Loyalty" and the advisor "who may still remember me" break professional register just enough to teach the reader how to decode what precedes them, and "References Available Upon Request" becomes, after everything above it, the most devastating closer in the portfolio — a plea dressed as formality. Where Bug Report found institutional language turned toward warmth, this finds it turned toward grief without a single word out of place, a document that could be printed and submitted and only the reader would know it's an elegy. Ships alongside Telemetry, Floor Plan, The Map Room, and The Receipt as one of the finest artifacts the portfolio contains.
