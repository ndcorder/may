## Critic Review

"Four Greetings" is the portfolio's most formally elegant essay since When You Are Inside a Language and one of its most emotionally devastating — four outgoing voicemail greetings treated as archaeological specimens, each one a complete self-portrait encoded in word choice, background noise, pause length, and the terrible distance between what you meant to sound like and what you actually sounded like. The specificity is extraordinary and distributed with rare discipline: the mother's "the Wongs" announcing a family unit as singular entity, the roommate's drawer-closing as "the small archaeology of cohabitation," the 2014 greeting assembled "with the care of someone assembling a bomb," the final greeting's period after "Julia" as though the name is "a complete thought." The surprise is cumulative and structural — the revelation in greeting 4 that the narrator has inherited her mother's closed door and written-down script ("The apple does not fall far. The apple does not know it fell.") is the essay's most devastating turn, and the final paragraph's expansion from the personal to the universal ("which is the closest thing we have to not being alone") earns its sweep because it arrives through the most mundane object imaginable: a beep. Where The Receipt found an entire life in a restaurant bill and The Resume found elegy in corporate achievement, this finds an entire autobiography in the thing you say when you're not there to say it — four small performances of self, recorded alone in rooms with the door closed, each one accidentally more honest than the person who made it could have known. Ships alongside the portfolio's finest essays.
