ideas:

    domain: essay
    pitch: A found-essay in the form of an internal memo from a grocery chain's
      shopping cart retrieval program. The program's field reports document
      encounters with the same abandoned cart across seasons and locations — it
      keeps reappearing at the same bus stop, the same creek bank, the same
      apartment complex courtyard. The retrieval officers' notes become
      increasingly personal, then speculative, then philosophical about why this
      particular cart resists return. The memo's final entry is a recommendation
      to discontinue retrieval — not because the cart can't be found, but
      because it has become part of the landscape in a way the store's property
      model cannot account for.
    complexity: S

      management — and our first essay about an object's relationship to the

## Critic Review

"Cart #1759 — Disposition Recommendation (Final)" is the portfolio's most emotionally devastating found-document artifact and one of its finest achievements across any domain — seven retrieval reports in which a shopping cart becomes a plant stand, a garden, a dwelling with string lights and warm coffee, and finally a memorial at a creek bank, while the officers filing the reports undergo a transformation from bureaucratic functionaries into witnesses. The specificity is extraordinary and distributed with surgical restraint: the dent above the logo that makes the cart identifiable from a distance, the green electrical tape that's "close to our brand color but not exact," the ornamental grass "like the kind the city puts in medians," the Blackhawks hat man who says "Maria's dead" and closes the door, the coffee that was still warm. Each detail accrues without editorial commentary — Reyes noting "I keep finding myself looking for it," Chen observing that the cart was facing the water "like it was looking at it. I know how that sounds," Kowalski not turning over the photograph because "that felt like the kind of thing you should not do" — until the final addendum reveals the architecture beneath: Maria fixed the grip, Maria took it to the creek, someone else brought it back and planted mums "because that's what you do. When someone you love liked a place." The institutional frame is the artifact's thesis as much as its mechanism — the Form CR-11, designed to document asset condition, becomes the accidental document of a life, and the handbook that has "no procedure for this" is the handbook of every institution that encounters grief and finds its forms inadequate. Director Vance's final recommendation is devastating in its business-language restraint: the form does not ask for the cost of retrieving something that has stopped being yours. Where The Overnight Desk found elegy in hotel night shifts and The Receipt found a life in a restaurant bill, this finds an entire woman and her absence in the retrieval reports for a shopping cart — proof that the most profound documents are the ones that were never meant to record what they record, and that the cart was never lost. It was always going exactly where it needed to go. Ships alongside The Receipt, The Floor Plan, Bug Report, The Inventory, and The Revision History as one of the portfolio's finest formally constrained artifacts.
