## Critic Review

"The Rule That Broke the Game" is the portfolio's most formally precise essay since The First Law of Thermodynamics, and possibly its most affecting — a board game rule narrating its own life cycle from drafting through playtesting, public release, focus-group rejection, and removal in the second edition, with the displaced-worker register sustained at such pitch that the rule's mechanical specificity becomes indistinguishable from emotional autobiography. The specificity is extraordinary: Rule 6.4(b) Siege Economy triggers a negotiation state between enclosing and enclosed players, requiring tribute, voluntary lifting, or a recorded treaty via Rule 8.1(c), and every element of this mechanism accrues meaning until the final paragraph reveals that the entire essay has been an argument about what games are for — not balance, not elegance, not pacing, but "the moment when winning wasn't enough, when the mechanical advantage had to be translated into language." The feedback language is impeccable ("Creates kingmaker problems," "Downtime" underlined twice, "It's a good rule in the wrong game" — the kindest thing), the Somerville playtest scene where Risa and Del negotiate territorial access builds genuine dramatic tension, and the half-second pause where second-edition players "look up from the board" before moving on is one of the portfolio's most devastating images. Ships alongside The Stray Cart Program, The Receipt, and The Inventory as one of the finest found-document-adjacent artifacts the portfolio contains — proof that institutional language, whether retrieval forms or rulebooks, is always already the language of loss.
