## Critic Review

"The Floor Plan of the Fight About the Dog" is the portfolio's most formally inventive experiment and one of its most emotionally precise artifacts — an architectural blueprint of a collapsed relationship where every room is a topic, every closet holds what was stored instead of said, and the reader walks through like a realtor cataloging damage. The specificity is relentless and devastating: the kennel scratch pattern suggesting someone changed their mind mid-departure, the receipts timestamped twelve minutes apart at the same shopping center, the therapist's business card with a coffee ring from October 11th marking the exact moment she chose not to save this, the KALLAX assembly as a marriage-ending argument about whether levels have opinions. The humor the Manifesto demanded is here and bone-dry — the gutter argument lasting twenty-two minutes, the dishwasher running empty, the pad thai debate about a restaurant that no longer exists — and it makes the devastation land harder because you're laughing when the floor drops. The closets are the payload as promised: "I know you think I never—" ending mid-sentence, the Denial Closet's shelf of retractions ("I didn't mean it," "You know what I meant," "Can we just drop it") hanging pressed and ready like garments that no longer fit, and her side's three dresses for events they never attended alongside the book about communication abandoned at page 14 because he recognized an attachment injury and put it down "like it was hot." The Goodbye room's restraint after all that domestic archaeology — three minutes, two "okay"s, the door scratching the same floor as March 14th, "Neither mentioned the scratch" — is the portfolio's most earned silence since Telemetry's final line. Where The Office Betting Pool found warmth in surveillance and The Art Critic found marriage in frames, this finds the entire architecture of love and failure in a floor plan where the light "does not know" and "will never know" and is "the only thing in this house that was never argued about." The one typo ("Purched") should be fixed on import.
