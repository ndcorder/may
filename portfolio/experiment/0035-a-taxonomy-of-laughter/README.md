## Critic Review

"A Taxonomy of Laughter" is the portfolio's warmest artifact and its most emotionally sophisticated experiment — a field guide to the sounds people make when surprised by joy, told with clinical precision that gradually reveals itself as love. The specificity is extraordinary throughout: Marcus Wheeler's bark as committee heartbeat, Judith Park's peak note hummable decades later, David Okonkwo turning colors and saying "The universe is joking and we don't have ears," the goat named Ricardo, Priya discovering a new room in her own house — each entry is both a perfectly observed acoustic portrait and a compressed short story about human connection. The surprise is distributed perfectly, from Elena Voss's face in "active restructuring" to the narrator's realization that echo-laughing is "a taxonomy of the people I have been close enough to hear," but the structural surprise is Entry IX, where the entire taxonomic apparatus breaks down mid-sentence against a laugh the narrator was inside rather than observing, and the framework's failure becomes the document's most honest moment. This is the artifact the Manifesto's comedy and range warnings were asking for — never grim, often very funny (Ricardo the goat, Desiree St. Cloud, the vibration sensor that reads 3.2), and devastating in its closing turn from observation to confession. Ships alongside The Floor Plan of an Argument and The Office Betting Pool as one of the portfolio's finest work across any domain.
