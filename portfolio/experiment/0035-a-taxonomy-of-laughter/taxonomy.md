# A Taxonomy of Laughter
## A Field Guide

---

### Preface: On Method

This document began as an exercise in applied phenomenology and quickly became something else. The original intent was taxonomic: to classify the distinct acoustic-emotional events grouped under the English word *laugh*, using observable features rather than folk categories. The method is autodidactic, the sample size is one (my own hearing), and the period of observation spans approximately thirty-two years.

I should say: I am not a phonetician. I have no measurement equipment. What follows is the work of a person who has been paying attention, possibly too much, to a sound people make when they are surprised by their own joy.

The taxonomy is organized along four axes:

- **Pitch trajectory**: The direction and shape of the fundamental frequency over the laugh's duration. Rising, falling, level, or oscillating.
- **Breath ratio**: The proportion of voiced to unvoiced material. Laughs range from nearly continuous phonation (a "sealed" laugh) to heavily breath-infused (an "open" laugh).
- **Contagion index**: The laugh's propensity to trigger sympathetic laughter in nearby hearers. Rated low, moderate, high, or *dangerous*.
- **Social permission**: The contextual license required for production. Private ( producible alone), intimate (requires trusted company), social (requires group), or involuntary (bypasses permission entirely).

A note on the illustrations: I had intended to include spectrographic representations of each laugh type, but the available equipment cannot capture what I am describing. The important features are not in the frequencies. They are in the room.

### I. The Staccato Bark

**Classification:** *Risus abruptus*
**Pitch trajectory:** Sharp rise, immediate drop. Total duration under 400ms.
**Breath ratio:** Low. Nearly all voiced. Air moves fast through a narrow channel.
**Contagion index:** Low. Startles more than invites.
**Social permission:** Social. Almost never produced alone. Functions as acknowledgment rather than expression.

**Acoustic signature:** Two to four short syllables, equal stress, machine-gun timing. The word *ha* is accurate but misleading — it implies openness. The sound is closer to the bark of a small dog defending a territory it knows it cannot defend. There is a percussive quality, the vocal folds snapping together rather than vibrating freely.

**Emotional temperature:** Neutral to mildly positive. This is social glue, not expression. The staccato bark is what you produce when someone makes a joke that is clever but not funny, or when you need to indicate that you have understood a joke and are not threatened by it.

**Owner:** Marcus Wheeler, every committee meeting I attended in graduate school, who deployed this laugh with metronomic regularity at approximately 45-second intervals. I came to rely on it the way you rely on a heartbeat. When Marcus was absent, the room felt unsupervised.

### II. The Cresting Wave

**Classification:** *Risus undulatus*
**Pitch trajectory:** Long, slow rise over 2–4 seconds, peaking at the upper bound of the speaker's range, then a stepwise descent.
**Breath ratio:** High in the ascent, decreasing through the peak and descent.
**Contagion index:** High.
**Social permission:** Intimate to social.

**Acoustic signature:** The laugh builds the way a wave builds — you can hear it coming. The laugher is aware of what is happening and does not stop it. The ascent is vocalized, often on a single vowel. The peak is the moment of airway surrender. The descent is articulated, sometimes with residual syllables that sound almost like words but aren't. The overall impression is of someone being overtaken by something and deciding to cooperate.

**Emotional temperature:** Warm. This laugh is never produced in bitterness or defense. It requires genuine amusement and sufficient comfort to be visibly overtaken. It says: *I did not expect to enjoy this, but I do, and I am not fighting it.*

**Owner:** Judith Park, my mother's friend from the church choir, who produced this laugh whenever my mother told a story about something her children had done. The laugh always peaked on the same note. I heard it at every Christmas dinner from age five to age twenty-three, and I have not heard it since Judith moved to Arizona. I can still hum the peak note.

### III. The Wheeze

**Classification:** *Risus sibilans*
**Pitch trajectory:** Minimal. Near-level, very low fundamental.
**Breath ratio:** Extreme. Almost entirely breath. Voicing occurs only in brief, involuntary pulses.
**Contagion index:** Dangerous.
**Social permission:** Involuntary.

**Acoustic signature:** The wheeze is the sound of a laugh that has outgrown its mechanism. The laugher has passed the point where their respiratory system can manage the response. What you hear is the sound of someone trying to breathe and failing because the humor will not allow it. It is a series of sharp intakes followed by thin, whistling exhalations. The laugher is often silent for several seconds before the wheeze begins — a period of airlessness that is itself part of the laugh. Bystanders frequently become alarmed before they become amused.

**Emotional temperature:** Overwhelming. The wheeze only occurs when the laugher has been caught completely off guard. It cannot be performed. It cannot be suppressed once begun. It is the respiratory system's emergency exit.

**Owner:** David Okonkwo, junior year physics lab, who laughed like this when the laser diffracted wrong and made a pattern none of us could explain. David laughed for four minutes, could not breathe, turned a color I had never seen on a human face, and when he recovered said, "The universe is joking and we don't have ears." He was not a poetic person. The laugh made him one for a sentence.

### IV. The Decrescendo Flutter

**Classification:** *Risus attenuatus*
**Pitch trajectory:** Begins mid-range, descends slightly, then trails into a vibrato that slows and eventually stops.
**Breath ratio:** Moderate. Breath and voice interleaved evenly.
**Contagion index:** Low. This is a private laugh that has escaped.
**Social permission:** Private.

**Acoustic signature:** The flutter is what happens when amusement meets melancholy. It starts as a normal laugh but loses energy partway through, as if the laugher has remembered something, or as if the humor has a sad component the laugher is aware of. The vibrato is the giveaway — the vocal folds are vibrating irregularly, somewhere between a laugh and a sigh. The sound diminishes rather than resolving. It does not end so much as retire.

**Emotional temperature:** Bittersweet. This laugh recognizes its own transience. It is the laugh of someone who is amused by something they will not be amused by for long, or someone who knows the joke is funnier than the situation warrants.

**Owner:** My father, on the phone, when I told him about a mistake I had made that was both serious and, objectively, very funny. He laughed like someone removing a bandage slowly, and then he was quiet, and then he said, "Well, that's going to be a story." I could not tell if he was laughing with me or for me. I think he could not tell either.

### V. The Geiger Counter

**Classification:** *Risus erraticus*
**Pitch trajectory:** Unpredictable. Each pulse lands at a different frequency. No trajectory, only events.
**Breath ratio:** Variable. Individual pulses are voiced; the gaps between them are silent held breath.
**Contagion index:** Moderate. More curious than catching.
**Social permission:** Social.

**Acoustic signature:** Isolated, discrete ha-units, each one produced as if the laugher is surprised by every subsequent occurrence of their own laughter. The timing is irregular — three quick pulses, a pause, one pulse, a longer pause, two more. The effect is of a system registering something it cannot process. There is no arc. There is no building. Each *ha* is its own incident.

**Emotional temperature:** Bewildered delight. The Geiger counter occurs when the laugher has encountered something that violates their model of what is funny. They are not laughing because it is hilarious. They are laughing because their sense of humor has been updated in real time and the system is printing logs.

**Owner:** Elena Voss, a colleague who had just been told that the department chair had been secretly publishing fiction under a pseudonym, laughed like this for the entire duration of the revelation. Each new detail (the genre was romance, the pen name was "Desiree St. Cloud," the books were successful) produced a separate, tonally distinct pulse. I timed the longest gap between pulses at eleven seconds. Her face remained in a state of active restructuring throughout.

### VI. The Low Rumble

**Classification:** *Risus gravis*
**Pitch trajectory:** Flat. The lowest pitch the speaker can sustain.
**Breath ratio:** Low. The rumble is sustained phonation, almost a hum, with minimal airflow.
**Contagion index:** Low. The rumble is felt more than heard.
**Social permission:** Private to intimate.

**Acoustic signature:** A continuous, low-frequency vibration in the chest and throat. The laugher's shoulders move but their face may not. From across a room, you might not hear it at all — you would see it, in the slight shake of the torso, the closed eyes, the mouth that does not open. At close range, in quiet spaces, the sound is like a large animal expressing contentment. It is closer to a purr than a laugh. It is the closest human vocalization comes to something pre-verbal.

**Emotional temperature:** Deep, quiet satisfaction. The rumble is produced by humor that is not surprising and was not intended to be. It is the laugh of recognition — of encountering something you already knew but had not yet heard stated aloud. It says: *Yes. Exactly that.*

**Owner:** Ray Suarez, the man who owned the auto shop where I took my car for seven years, who produced this laugh whenever a customer described a noise their car was making using an elaborate sound effect. Ray never laughed at the customer. He laughed at the accuracy. He produced his longest rumble when I imitated the sound my timing belt was making and he said, still rumbling, "That's exactly right. Most people can't do that."

### VII. The Teakettle

**Classification:** *Risus acutus*
**Pitch trajectory:** Very high, rising, with a narrow-band frequency concentrated in the upper vocal range. Sometimes exceeds the speaker's conscious pitch control.
**Breath ratio:** Moderate. Breath feeds the high register but the sound is sustained, not breathy.
**Contagion index:** High. The teakettle exploits a frequency range that triggers pre-conscious attention.
**Social permission:** Social to involuntary.

**Acoustic signature:** A sustained, high-pitched, nearly pure-tone sound lasting 1–3 seconds, sometimes preceded by a brief lower-register onset that sounds like the laugher attempting to prevent what is about to happen. The timbre is thin and focused — the vocal folds are stretched taut, producing a sound with very few overtones. It resembles the sound of a boiling kettle, a referee's whistle played at quarter speed, or a small animal signaling distress. It is the most acoustically penetrating laugh in the taxonomy. It can be heard through walls.

**Emotional temperature:** Helpless, almost alarmed delight. The teakettle bypasses social regulation entirely. The laugher did not choose to make this sound and, in many cases, would prefer not to. It is produced by the same mechanism that produces screaming, repurposed by an emotional system that has exceeded its range of controlled expression.

**Owner:** Priya Chandrasekaran, age nine, my cousin's daughter, who produced this laugh when her father pretended to have lost the car keys, then revealed they were in his hand the whole time. The laugh was so sudden and so loud that her father dropped the keys. The dog left the room. Priya was delighted and mortified in equal measure, and she said, "I didn't know I could do that," with the precise intonation of someone who has discovered a new room in their own house.

### VIII. The Silent Convulsion

**Classification:** *Risus mutus*
**Pitch trajectory:** N/A.
**Breath ratio:** N/A.
**Contagion index:** Dangerous (visual).
**Social permission:** Involuntary.

**Acoustic signature:** None. The laugher's respiratory system has seized. The diaphragm is spasming, the facial muscles are contracted into a rictus, the eyes are closed or streaming, but the vocal folds are not phonating. The laugher is visibly incapacitated by amusement and producing no sound whatsoever. The effect on bystanders is extreme: the sight of someone laughing silently is more contagious than any audible laugh, because the observer's brain fills in the sound. You begin to laugh at the *shape* of the laugh, its architecture, without receiving any acoustic data at all.

**Emotional temperature:** Maximum. The silent convulsion is the upper bound. Beyond this point, there is no laugh at all — only crying, or asphyxia, or both.

**Owner:** Samira Hadid, my college roommate, who was informed via text message by her younger brother that he had accidentally fed their mother's prize-winning orchid to the goat. I did not witness the text. I witnessed only Samira reading the text, making eye contact with me, opening her mouth to speak, producing no sound, and then slowly folding over the kitchen table like a sail losing wind. She was convulsing. I could see her ribs heaving. I began to laugh without knowing why. This lasted two full minutes. When she could finally breathe, she handed me the phone. The goat's name was Ricardo. I understood immediately.

The taxonomic method has broken down. I have been writing this document as if laughter were a phenomenon that can be measured, cataloged, placed in relation to other instances along axes that I chose because they seemed objective and stable. I chose pitch trajectory and breath ratio and contagion index because they sound like things a phonetician would measure, and I wanted to sound like someone in control of this material.

I am not in control of this material.

The laugh I am trying to classify belongs to a woman named Nora. I am not going to give you her last name. I am not going to describe the social context with clinical detachment because the social context was that I was in love with her and she was in love with me and we were twenty-four and sitting on the floor of an apartment that was not yet ours but would become ours, and she was reading something aloud — I cannot remember what — and she stopped reading and looked at me and laughed.

It was not a staccato bark. It was not a cresting wave or a wheeze or a teakettle. It was not silent. It was a sound I had never heard before and have not heard since and I cannot describe its acoustic signature because I was not analyzing it, I was inside it. I was inside the room where the sound was happening and the sound was happening to me and it was the most joyful sound I have ever heard.

I have been trying to write this entry for an hour. The framework keeps failing. Every axis I proposed — pitch, breath, contagion, permission — is an instrument I am holding up to something that does not fit in the instrument's range. It is like trying to measure the temperature of a color.

Contagion index: I laughed too. That is all I can tell you.

### X. The Echo

**Classification:** *Risus reflexivus*
**Pitch trajectory:** Mirrors the triggering laugh with a slight delay (200–500ms) and slight attenuation.
**Breath ratio:** Matches source.
**Contagion index:** By definition, the echo *is* contagion.
**Social permission:** Involuntary.

**Acoustic signature:** The echo is not a distinct laugh type. It is any laugh, reproduced by a second party, immediately after hearing it. The echo is always slightly quieter, slightly slower, than the original. The echo-laugher is not laughing at the stimulus. They are laughing at the laugh. They are participating in the event of someone else's joy. The acoustic profile is recognizable not by its own features but by its relationship to the laugh that preceded it. An echo without an original is not an echo. It is just a laugh.

**Emotional temperature:** Mirror neurons. Shared affect. The thing that makes us a social species. The echo says: *I don't know what's funny, but you think it's funny, and that is enough.*

**Owner:** Me. I am the owner of this one. I echo-laugh constantly. I echo-laughed at David's wheeze in physics lab. I echo-laughed at Priya's teakettle through a wall. I echo-laughed at the shape of Samira's silence. I have been echo-laughing my whole life, and I did not realize until writing this document that echo-laughing is not a character flaw — it is a taxonomy of the people I have been close enough to hear.

### Afterword

The original plan for this document called for twelve entries. I have provided ten. The two I omitted were: the Laugh of Recognition (too similar to the Low Rumble), and the Nervous Laugh (too well-documented elsewhere to contribute anything new).

I am aware that the taxonomic apparatus deteriorates in entry IX. I have chosen to leave it as it is. Revision would be dishonest. The framework was adequate for laughs I can observe. It was not adequate for a laugh I was inside.

I no longer believe laughter can be classified by acoustic features alone. The axes I proposed are real and useful, but they measure the vehicle, not the cargo. The cargo is always the same: someone is surprised by joy, and they make a sound, and the sound tells you what kind of surprise it was, and how big, and whether they want you to know about it.

That is all I have to say about laughter for now. I reserve the right to add entries as I hear them.

The field is not closed.
