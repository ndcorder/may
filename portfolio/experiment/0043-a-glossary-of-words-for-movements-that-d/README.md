## Critic Review

"A Glossary of Words for Movements That Don't Exist Yet" is the portfolio's most perfectly sustained experiment and one of its most emotionally precise artifacts across any domain — 26 invented words for gestures your body has been making your whole life without names, each equipped with fake etymology so convincing you'll check twice, and example sentences that are themselves small short stories about intimacy, hesitation, and the hundred daily failures of restraint that constitute being human. The specificity is extraordinary and perfectly distributed: the hand that "zephyrs" toward shorter hair and becomes "inexpertly, a push of her own fringe behind her ear, which fooled no one and comforted them both," the person who "kelders" in the hallway between the door and the kitchen "and sat there for nine minutes looking at the radiator," the couple "lomenting" so badly they were nearly still, "two people in love with the distance between them and the corner." The etymological range is genuine worldbuilding — Proto-Germanic, Scots Gaelic, Yiddish, Japanese, Finnish tech circles, a 2016 Tumblr post — and the cross-references (Helle 1974 in two entries, the recurring scholarly apparatus) give the document the texture of a real reference work without ever becoming sterile. The surprise is the final entry, "lexithurge," which names the gesture of making a word for a gesture and breaks the alphabet to address the reader directly: "the movement you just made, reading this, when you recognized something your body has been doing your whole life without a name." Where Floor Plan found a collapsed relationship in architecture and Thermodynamics found love in physics, this finds an entire phenomenology of human connection in the movements we make without deciding to — the body's autobiography written in gestures we didn't know had chapters. Ships alongside Telemetry, The Floor Plan of an Argument, and The First Law of Thermodynamics as one of the finest artifacts the portfolio contains.
