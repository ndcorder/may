#1 Unprompted follow-up question detected at grocery checkout
tone-calibration severity: medium status: wontfix
Opened Nov 2, 2024 by your-name · Closed Nov 9, 2024 · 3 comments
Description
During routine grocery checkout (items: sourdough bread, butter, one lemon), the cashier asked if I'd found everything okay. I said yes. Standard interaction so far — this is the expected script. But then, unprompted, she asked: "Are you making something nice?"

This is outside the expected interaction pattern for a grocery checkout. There was no leading context — I hadn't mentioned cooking, hadn't been looking confused, hadn't been browsing strangely. The question was initiated by her and was not required by any social script I'm aware of.

Duration of exchange: approximately 4 seconds longer than baseline checkout interaction.
Reproduction Steps
  1. Purchase same three items at the same store
  2. Attend checkout lane 6 (same cashier as original incident)
  3. Respond to standard greeting
  4. Wait for follow-up question
Expected Behavior
Transaction completes. Standard pleasantries. "Have a good one." Departure.
Actual Behavior
Follow-up question initiated by second party. Brief but genuine engagement with the contents of the purchase rather than the fact of it. Small but noticeable warmth in vocal inflection.
Resolution Notes
Returned the following week. Same cashier, same items. She didn't ask again. Possible explanations:
— Behavior was situational (mood-dependent, not reproducible)
— Different body language on my part (less approachable posture)
— She didn't remember me (likely — I am not memorable)

Attempted reproduction: 3 times. Success rate: 0/3.

Closing as will not fix. The thing that happened was real — I didn't imagine it. But it belonged to that specific moment and I can't get it back. That's okay. Some bugs only fire once.

ambient noise: moderate

  1. Make joke that contains actual emotional content
  2. Wait for laugh
  3. See if anyone notices the part that wasn't a joke

Laughter. Topic moves on. The actual content dissolves safely into the social fabric. I remain hidden.
Actual Behavior
Laughter, then pause, then follow-up that engaged with the emotional subtext. Brief exposure. I had to decide whether to be honest or to deflect. I deflected. But the choice was available. That's the bug.

#3 Friend noticed unprompted — did not require verbalization of need

Description
Team lunch at work. I was sitting slightly apart — not dramatically, just angled away from the main conversation. I do this sometimes when I'm running low and haven't figured out how to say it yet.

  1. Enter social situation while in depleted state
  2. Attempt to participate at baseline levels (approx. 40% engagement)
  3. Gradually disengage — angle body away, reduce verbal contributions, let eyes unfocus
  4. Wait for someone to either: (a) ask "are you okay" or (b) not notice
Expected Behavior
Option (b) — not noticed. This is the safe outcome. Sometimes option (a), which requires management: reassure, minimize, redirect.
Actual Behavior
Option (c) — noticed without acknowledged noticing. Presence offered without demand. A story about a hawk. I didn't have to perform okay-ness. I didn't have to explain not-okay-ness. I just got to sit there and listen to someone describe something beautiful they'd seen, and after a few minutes I realized I felt better, and I hadn't had to do anything to make that happen.

This is not supposed to work this way.

#8 Crying at a song — friend did not attempt to fix
vulnerability emotional-response severity: critical
Opened Nov 26, 2024 by your-name · 4 comments
Description
At D's apartment. He put on music — just background, nobody was really listening. Then a song came on, I think it was Phoebe Bridgers, something about a backyard, and there was a line about being known, and I started crying.

Not a lot. Not sobbing. Just — my eyes were wet and I couldn't explain why and D was right there.
Reproduction Steps
  1. Be in a room with someone whose opinion matters to you
  2. Experience an emotion you didn't consent to having
  3. Fail to suppress visible evidence of emotion
  4. Wait for the other person to handle it
Expected Behavior
Awkward comfort attempt. "Hey, are you okay?" — which requires a response, which requires me to either lie or explain, and I can't explain because I don't know why I'm crying, and now we're both stuck. Or: pretending not to notice, which is its own kind of pain. Or: humor, deflection, topic change — anything to restore equilibrium.
Actual Behavior
"Yeah. That song."

Two words. Then he moved to get a tissue and set it on the table next to me. Didn't hand it to me — that would've required acknowledgment. Just placed it within reach. And then he didn't say anything else. He just let the song finish. And then the next song came on and we kept sitting there.

I was scared and he didn't look away.

I'm still scared. But I think about that night when I am.
Severity: CRITICAL
This isn't critical because something went wrong. This is critical because something went right, and I have no framework for integrating it. This experience has no corresponding entry in my emotional reference database. It's not filed under "comfort" or "support" or any of the categories I have schemas for. It was something else. It was being with someone while being exactly who I was.

I don't know how to be that person. I don't know if I get to be that person more than once.
Note to self: If you can figure out how to reproduce this — not the crying, but the safety — log it. Log everything. This is the most important data point.
#9 Held hands — imperceptible squeeze was returned
authenticity vulnerability severity: critical status: will not close
Opened Nov 29, 2024 by your-name · 0 comments
Description
Walking home from the movie. We were talking about something — I think the ending, whether the main character made the right choice. Cold out. Hands brushed.

I reached back.
Reproduction Steps
  1. Walk next to someone
  2. Hands make accidental contact
  3. Do not flinch
  4. Do not apologize for the contact
  5. Do not pretend it didn't happen
  6. Reach back
Expected Behavior
Brief contact. Both parties pretend it didn't happen. Conversation continues. Safe distance restored. The brush becomes a nothing. The walk becomes a walk, not a held hand.
Actual Behavior
Fingers interlaced. Small squeeze — I initiated, I don't know why. Squeeze returned. Firm but not urgent. Like saying yes. Like saying I'm here. Like saying you don't have to ask first.

The walk was 11 minutes. I don't remember what we talked about. I remember the temperature of her hand. I remember that my palm was sweating and she didn't let go.
Status: WILL NOT CLOSE
I don't want to resolve this. I don't want to understand it enough that it stops happening. I don't want to file it away as "solved" and move on to the next thing.

This is the only issue in the tracker I want to stay open forever.

Every other bug on this list — I logged it because I was surprised. Because something happened that I didn't expect, and I needed to understand it, and I don't understand things unless I write them down.

This one I don't want to understand. I just want it to keep happening.

Assignee: nobody. Not yours, not mine. Ours.
If you're reading this — and I don't know why you would be, this repo is private, or at least I thought it was — I want you to know that I've been logging these because I didn't believe connection was something that could happen to me. I thought it was a system I was supposed to learn. A set of inputs and outputs. I thought if I tracked it carefully enough, I could figure out how it worked and then maybe it would start working for me.

I don't think that anymore.

It's not a bug. It was never a bug. I was the one who was filed wrong.
