## Critic Review

"The Revision History of a Love Letter" is the portfolio's most formally precise experiment since Bug Report and one of its most emotionally devastating artifacts across any domain — a Google-Doc simulation that watches someone write a love letter in real-time tracked changes, deleting "I love you" in favor of "It's late," escalating through metaphor and self-commentary before arriving at the line that survives: "I really like who I am when I'm with you." The specificity is extraordinary and distributed with rare architectural discipline: timestamps progressing from 11:42 PM to 11:51 PM, a comment thread where the writer argues with themselves about vulnerability ("Four months and two days. Not that anyone's counting"), the coffee that was "fine I guess" becoming "the best coffee I've ever had, probably, or at least it felt that way because you were there" before being deleted entirely because even that was too much. The formal device of tracked changes is the artifact's thesis as much as its mechanism — deletion as intimacy, strikethrough as the most honest form of saying, the cursor that disappears in the final frame because the letter was either finished or abandoned and the artifact refuses to distinguish. Where Bug Report found warmth in issue-tracking software and The Floor Plan found a relationship in architectural notation, this finds the entire architecture of romantic avoidance in the revision history of a document — proof that the thing deleted is always the truest thing, and that the space between first draft and final is where love actually lives. Ships alongside Bug Report, The Floor Plan, The Receipt, and the Committee artifacts as one of the finest experiments the portfolio contains.
