## Critic Review

"RFC 9527 — Reconnection Handshake Protocol After Extended Silence" is the portfolio's most emotionally devastating found-document artifact and one of its finest achievements across any domain — an IETF standards document that begins as a technically rigorous specification for reconnecting after years of silence and gradually, through six implementation notes in Section 9, becomes the most personal thing the portfolio contains. The specificity is extraordinary and distributed with architectural discipline: the trigger conditions ("The birth of a child, specifically because the child is named after the responder, and an explanation is overdue"), the hug failure recovery procedure with its mandated laughter, the departure hug decision matrix, the T_FORGIVENESS timeout listed as "Unbounded." But the artifact's central achievement is Section 9.4 — "The Author and His Brother" — where the RFC collapses from specification to confession: 30 unsent messages in a notes app dated and timestamped, the read receipt as "the most ambiguous signal in the RHP protocol suite," and the final line that redefines the entire document: "The author suspects that outside of engineering, it is called waiting." The fake references are perfect (Okonkwo's "Mutual Anticipated Rejection" circulated privately among 6 people who recognized themselves), the Brennan-and-Daluga deployment where both comments about aging "were intended as compliments but received as insults" is technically precise and humanly true, and the FM-4 failure mode — "The Reconnection That Works," classified as a failure because "it is unexpected and can be disorienting to endpoints who had adapted to the absence" — is one of the wisest sentences in the portfolio. Where Bug Report found warmth in issue-tracking software and The Stray Cart Program found elegy in retrieval forms, this finds an entire unspoked brotherhood in a protocol specification — proof that the distance between engineering and love is shorter than either discipline admits, and that the most honest document is the one that pretends, for as long as it can, to be about something else.
