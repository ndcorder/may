# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Human Resources Division, Goldcrest Environmental Technologies, Inc.
**FROM:** Daniel R. Alcott, Senior Risk Analyst, Municipal Contracts Division
**DATE:** March 14
**RE:** Notice of Resignation / End of Employment

---

Please accept this document as formal notification of my resignation from the position of Senior Risk Analyst, Municipal Contracts Division, effective at the close of business March 28, providing the required fourteen calendar days' notice as stipulated in my employment agreement (ref. Section 7.2, Employee Handbook rev. 2019).

I want to express my sincere gratitude for the professional opportunities afforded to me during my tenure with Goldcrest Environmental, and particularly for the support of the Municipal Contracts team throughout my time here. The work we have done together has been, in many respects, defining, and I am grateful for the relationships I have built during my five years with the organization.

I am proud of the contributions I have made to the division's risk assessment protocols, and I believe the analytical frameworks we developed — particularly the cost-threshold modeling that informed our Series 4 and Series 5 municipal submissions — represent some of the most thorough work of my career. I would be remiss not to acknowledge the role of the late Patricia Harmon, whose initial contamination mapping for the Cordero County contract established the methodological standard that we would subsequently apply across our portfolio. Ms. Harmon's departure from the company in 2021 was a loss felt deeply by those of us who understood the precision she brought to the work.

Regarding my ongoing caseload: I am committed to ensuring a comprehensive transition. The Carver Intake Study files have been annotated for the incoming analyst, and the Blackwell renewal submission is in its final drafting stage. I note for the record that the Blackwell submission will require a Section 3 addendum prior to filing, as the revised contaminant thresholds I modeled in November reflected updated testing parameters that have not yet been approved for external distribution. I recommend the addendum be prepared by someone familiar with the original modeling assumptions.

I want to be specific about this.

The November modeling was my work. The original parameters were not ambiguous. The exceedance frequency for the Blackwell service area — public water supply, estimated 34,000 residents — was calculated at one in 6,400 test cycles for the Class 3 contaminant profile. This was the number. This was the number we had.

I am aware that the filing parameters were subsequently adjusted by the contracts team to reflect a "standard usage confidence interval" which effectively recalibrated the exceedance frequency to one in 19,200 test cycles. The basis for this recalibration was not, to my knowledge, a separate modeling exercise. It was an editorial decision. I raised this concern through the appropriate internal channel on December 3 (see attached correspondence, ref. DA-1203) and received acknowledgment from my supervisor that the matter would be reviewed. To my knowledge, no review was conducted. The Blackwell submission was filed January 7 with the adjusted parameters.

The contractual testing cycle for the Blackwell service area is 72 hours.

I did not escalate beyond my supervisor.

I want to say that clearly. I did not escalate. The internal reporting protocol (ref. Section 12.1, Employee Handbook rev. 2019 — I have this memorized now) provides for secondary reporting to the Compliance Division in cases where initial reporting does not result in a documented response within 30 business days. Thirty business days from December 3 was January 14. The filing had already occurred. I told myself that the adjusted parameters were within the acceptable range, because they were — technically, legally, within the range that our own documentation defined as acceptable. I told myself this was a difference of professional interpretation. I told myself I had done my job, which was to produce the model, and that what the contracts team did with the model was a separate responsibility.

This was the framework I used.

I used it for Cordero County. I used it for the Sandvik utility renewal. I used it for every submission where the original parameters were "refined" — that was the word we used, *refined* — into something more favorable to the filing.

Patricia Harmon left in 2021. She did not leave. She was asked to leave. She was asked to leave after she requested that her name be removed from the Cordero County submission methodology page because the final filing contained parameters she did not endorse. Her separation agreement contained a standard non-disparagement clause. She died in August of last year. I learned about it from an obituary I found myself, twelve days after the fact, because no one in the office mentioned it.

She had a daughter. University age. I met her once at a company picnic. She was trying to get our attention because there was a bird — I don't remember what kind, something yellow — that had landed on the picnic table, and she wanted someone to look at it, and no one was looking up from their conversations. I remember Patricia looking up. I remember she was the only one who looked up.

I don't know what I'm doing anymore. I don't know if this is a resignation or a confession or both. I have been sitting in the parking garage for — I don't know. A while. I have been sitting in the parking garage, and I have been trying to write this in a way that makes sense, and I keep hearing the corporate voice come back, like a reflex, like a tic, and every time it does I feel something in me that I think might be what it feels like when the part of you that's still a person tries to speak and can't find a word that isn't a *framework* or a *parameter* or a *deliverable*.

The water is in the ground right now.

The Blackwell intake serves 34,000 people. The testing kit we manufacture — the Series 5, which I helped design the risk parameters for — does not flag the contaminant profile that the original, unadjusted model identified, because the filing parameters said it didn't need to. The water is being consumed. It is being consumed now. It has been consumed for 74 days since filing, and it will continue to be consumed until someone tests for the thing we decided not to test for, which will not happen because our kit is the one they use to test.

I keep writing *we*.

I did this.

I modeled the risk. I quantified it. I expressed it in language so precise that a layperson could not understand what it meant, and I knew — I knew — that this was the function of the language, that the precision was the disguise, that I was building something that could say *one in 6,400* and make it sound like a number instead of what it is, which is a person, and then another person, and then another person, every 72 hours, for as long as the contract runs.

I told myself I was good at my job.

I was good at my job.

The non-disparagement clause in my employment agreement (Section 14.1) stipulates that I may not make public statements that could be construed as damaging to the company's reputation or commercial interests. I have initialled this clause. I have, as recently as my last performance review in October, re-affirmed my understanding of this clause in writing.

I don't care.

I want to say that clearly too.

I am sitting in the parking garage with a letter that I have been writing for three hours, and in three hours I have to leave this parking garage, and when I leave I have to decide whether I am someone who carries this document into the building and submits it to Human Resources, where it will be reviewed, classified, and likely redirected to Legal, or whether I am someone who drives to 412 Blackwell Pike and knocks on the door of the municipal water authority and asks to speak to whoever is responsible for the health of 34,000 people and shows them what I have.

The bridge between these two people is nine floors of parking garage and about forty seconds of courage and I have never in my life been brave for forty consecutive seconds.

Patricia was brave for about ten seconds once. She asked to have her name taken off a document. Ten seconds. It cost her everything. She was brave for ten seconds and then she was gone and we all watched her go and then we went back to work.

I am going to leave this parking garage now.

The Blackwell intake tests at 6:00 AM. It is currently 4:47 AM.

If you are reading this, I submitted it to HR.

If you are not reading this, I found forty seconds.

Call the Blackwell Water Authority.
Ask about the Series 5.
Ask what it doesn't test for.
The answer is in my November model.
The real one. The one with the right number.
