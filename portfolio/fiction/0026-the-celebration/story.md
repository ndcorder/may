**BARCLAY VOLUNTEER AMBULANCE CORPS**
**EVENT SHIFT LOG — September 15**

**Crew A (0800–1400):** T. Novick (EMT-P), K. Sadowski (EMT-B)
**Crew B (1400–2200):** J. Mangahas (EMT-P), R. Vega (EMT-B)

**0840** — On site. Staged at Maple and 4th per event plan. Carried jump bags, AED, trauma kit. Radio check with Capt. Herrera — confirmed.

**0900–1140** — Minor calls only. Bee sting (left forearm, boy ~8, observed 30 min, released), laceration (thumb, corn dog skewer, bandaged, refused transport — this is the third year this person has cut herself on a corn dog and refused transport. Recognized her. Told her to at least wash it, she said she would), heat assessment (elderly male, cooling tent, vitals stable).

**1215** — Dispatched to tether area. Report of a "structural issue." Thought it was the bounce house again. En route, Herrera on radio: "All available units to the square. I need the ambulance at the square NOW."

**1217** — Arrived on scene. 

I don't know how to write this part for a log, so I'm going to write it for a log.

The basket was upright on the square. The envelope was on the gazebo and the bandshell and the cotton candy booth and the maple tree by Dr. Pham's office. Propane tank was still hissing. Fire suppression was active. Herrera had the pilot out and on the ground, being treated by one of his firefighters for burns to both forearms. The two crew members were clear, minor injuries — one had skinned knees and hands and was crying, the other was just standing there.

There were people under the envelope. The envelope is not light when it's full of hot air and not rising. The cotton candy vendor and a woman I didn't know were holding up a section of it, and there was screaming underneath. 

K. went in under the fabric. I went in after. It was very hot and very dark and I couldn't see. There was a lot of fabric. We found the child by sound.

**1224** — Patient: female, approximately 5–6 years old, extricated from under envelope material. Conscious, crying. Abrasions to face and arms, possible fracture left radius, singed hair. Attempted to shield her from the — I'm not going to describe the scene. She could see it. I picked her up and turned her around and carried her out.

**1228** — Second patient: male, approximately 45, under envelope material near tether point. Bystanders attempting to free him. I called for additional hands. 

I'm going to stop writing the times. This isn't a log.

— T. Novick
