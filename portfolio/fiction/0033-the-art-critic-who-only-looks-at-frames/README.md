## Critic Review

"Frame of Reference" is the portfolio's most sustained feat of constrained perception and one of its most emotionally devastating artifacts — eight gallery reviews written by a critic who attends entirely to frames, hardware, walls, floors, and the bodies of other viewers, building a portrait of marriage, rivalry, loneliness, and attention so complete that the absent paintings become irrelevant long before the final review removes them entirely. The specificity is extraordinary and functional throughout: quarter-inch hex heads catching gallery lights at consistent angles, rabbet depth suggesting panel rather than canvas, bole-red underneath proper gilding with cast rather than carved compo ornament — each detail extends character and theme simultaneously, establishing Maren as someone whose refusal to look at the obvious is itself the most demanding form of seeing. The surprise is distributed with rare precision: Elena's hand on the shoulder in the Yun Ok-Seon review, Marcus's "They're not coming back," the narrator's admission that Elena's voice did something on the second syllable of "conference" that she could spend a month mapping — each lands without announcement, and the cumulative portrait of a marriage conducted through gallery visits is devastating because it's never explained, only accumulated. The final review earns every page of setup: the empty room with its D-rings shaped like question marks, the spackle yellowed like scars older than injuries, Elena walking in and saying just "Oh" — and the closing line "They were full" is the portfolio's best final sentence since Telemetry's "everybody knew and nobody said." Where The Reviewer Who Loved Everything found grace in restaurant criticism and The Celebration found comedy in municipal paperwork, this finds its entire emotional register in the architecture of display, and makes a convincing case that how we hold things matters as much as what we hold. This is the artifact the Manifesto's range and surprise warnings were asking for, and it ships alongside Exit Interview, Telemetry, and The Office Betting Pool as one of the portfolio's finest fictions.
