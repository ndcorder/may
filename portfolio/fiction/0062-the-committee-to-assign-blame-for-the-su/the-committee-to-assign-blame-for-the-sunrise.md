# THE COMMITTEE TO ASSIGN BLAME FOR THE SUNRISE
## Special Committee Minutes
### Town of Meridian — Municipal Building, Room 4C
**Approved for Public Record**

---

# MEETING 1

**Date:** September 9th
**Time Called to Order:** 7:14 PM
**Presiding:** Councilwoman Diane Glover, Ward 3
**Recording Secretary:** Marcus Fell, Town Clerk's Office
**Members Present:** Diane Glover (Chair), Robert Kwon (Vice Chair), Sandra Mills, Patricia Coale, James Hoadley, Thomas Rael
**Members Absent:** None
**Quorum:** Established (6 of 6)

**Opening Remarks:**

Councilwoman Glover called the meeting to order at 7:14 PM and read the enabling resolution (Resolution 2024-441: "To Establish a Special Committee for the Investigation and Adjudication of the Sunrise Phenomenon"). She noted the resolution was passed by Town Council on September 2nd with a vote of 5-2, with Councilmen Brewer and Ng dissenting.

Councilwoman Glover then asked if there was any objection to the proposed committee name: "The Committee to Assign Blame for the Sunrise." Mr. Kwon asked whether "responsibility" might be preferable to "blame," as it sounded more neutral. Councilwoman Glover agreed the suggestion had merit but noted that the enabling resolution specifically used the word "blame" and that the committee was bound by that language unless they petitioned Council for an amendment. Mrs. Mills moved to accept the name as written. Motion seconded by Mr. Hoadley. Motion carried 5-1, with Mr. Kwon opposed.

**Swearing In:**

Town Clerk Marcus Fell administered the oath of office to all six committee members. Each member signed the Committee Membership Register.

**Election of Officers:**

Councilwoman Glover noted that she had been designated as temporary Chair per the enabling resolution. She opened the floor for nominations for permanent Chair. Mrs. Mills nominated Councilwoman Glover. Mr. Rael seconded. No other nominations. Councilwoman Glover elected Chair by acclamation.

For Vice Chair, Mr. Hoadley nominated Mr. Kwon. Mrs. Coale seconded. No other nominations. Mr. Kwon elected Vice Chair by acclamation.

**Adoption of Bylaws:**

Councilwoman Glover presented draft bylaws for the committee's governance. The bylaws specified:

1. Regular meetings shall be held weekly on Monday evenings.
2. A quorum shall consist of four members.
3. The Chair shall set the agenda but any member may add items with a second.
4. Minutes shall be kept by the Town Clerk's office and made available to the public within five business days.
5. All votes shall be recorded in the minutes.

Mr. Rael asked whether there was a termination clause — how would the committee know when its work was done? Councilwoman Glover acknowledged this was a good question but noted the bylaws could be amended at any regular meeting. Mr. Hoadley suggested the committee would know its work was finished when it had completed its work, which he acknowledged was circular but felt was appropriate given the nature of the charge. This remark was not recorded as a formal motion.

Mrs. Coale moved to adopt the bylaws as presented. Motion seconded by Mr. Rael. Motion carried 6-0.

**Discussion Item 1: Scope of Investigation**

Councilwoman Glover opened discussion on the committee's mandate. She read from the enabling resolution: "The Committee shall investigate, examine, and determine responsibility for the daily occurrence of sunrise within the Town of Meridian and its immediate environs, and shall make recommendations to the Town Council regarding appropriate accountability measures."

Mr. Rael asked what "immediate environs" meant. Was the committee's jurisdiction limited to sunrises visible from within town limits, or did it extend to any sunrise that affected town residents? Councilwoman Glover said this was a question they would need to settle.

Mrs. Mills said she believed the committee's focus should be on the sunrise itself — the event — rather than on any particular viewing of it. The sun rose whether anyone in Meridian saw it or not. That was precisely what made it suspicious.

Mr. Kwon asked whether the committee was seriously proposing to investigate a natural phenomenon. Councilwoman Glover reminded him that the Town Council had established the committee by majority vote, and that the committee's role was not to question its own existence but to fulfill its mandate.

Mr. Hoadley noted that the sunrise happened every day without fail and had done so for the entire recorded history of the town and, as far as he knew, the entire history of the world. He said that anything that reliable deserved investigation, because it was either being managed by someone very competent or it was operating outside of anyone's control entirely, and he did not care for the second option.

Mrs. Coale said she had looked into the matter and found that the Town of Meridian had no existing ordinance, resolution, or policy regarding the sunrise. It occurred each day without any authorization, oversight, or accountability mechanism whatsoever. She found this concerning.

Mr. Rael moved that the committee define its scope to include: (a) the cause or causes of the sunrise, (b) any person or persons responsible for it, (c) whether the town had any regulatory authority over it, and (d) what if anything should be done about it. Motion seconded by Mrs. Mills. Motion carried 6-0.

**Discussion Item 2: Invitation of Expert Testimony**

Mrs. Mills suggested the committee invite expert witnesses to speak at future meetings. She proposed starting with a meteorologist or astronomer who could explain the mechanical process of the sunrise.

Mr. Kwon asked whether they were looking for an explanation or for someone to hold accountable. Mrs. Mills said she did not see why those had to be different things.

Councilwoman Glover directed the secretary to contact the astronomy department at the community college and the local meteorologist, Mr. Frank Burrell, to invite them to Meeting 2.

**Public Comment:**

No members of the public were present.

**Adjournment:**

Mrs. Mills moved to adjourn. Motion seconded by Mr. Hoadley. Meeting adjourned at 9:02 PM.

**Next Meeting:** September 16th, 7:00 PM, Room 4C.

# MEETING 2

**Date:** September 16th
**Time Called to Order:** 7:03 PM
**Presiding:** Councilwoman Diane Glover, Chair
**Recording Secretary:** Marcus Fell, Town Clerk's Office
**Members Present:** Diane Glover, Robert Kwon, Sandra Mills, Patricia Coale, Thomas Rael
**Members Absent:** James Hoadley (excused)
**Quorum:** Established (5 of 6)

**Approval of Previous Minutes:**

Mrs. Coale noted a typographical error on page 2, line 14, where "phenomenon" was misspelled. Mr. Fell acknowledged the error and indicated it would be corrected in the final version. Minutes approved as amended 5-0.

**Public Comment:**

One member of the public was present: Mr. Howard Peel, 48 Maple Street. Mr. Peel asked whether the committee was "actually serious" and, if so, whether they understood how they appeared to the broader community. Councilwoman Glover thanked Mr. Peel for his comments. Mr. Peel remained seated for the duration of the meeting but did not speak again.

**Guest Testimony: Frank Burrell, Meteorologist (Retired)**

Mr. Burrell was sworn in by the secretary. Councilwoman Glover thanked him for attending and asked him to explain, in terms the committee could understand, what caused the sunrise.

Mr. Burrell stated that the Earth rotates on its axis approximately once every twenty-four hours, and that any given point on the surface moves into and out of the sun's light during that rotation. Sunrise, he said, was the moment when a particular location rotated into the sun's light. It was a consequence of planetary mechanics.

Mr. Rael asked what caused the Earth to rotate. Mr. Burrell said it was angular momentum remaining from the formation of the solar system approximately 4.6 billion years ago.

Mrs. Mills asked if anyone had been in charge of that formation. Mr. Burrell said no, not as far as he understood it. It was a natural process.

Mrs. Coale asked whether Mr. Burrell found it suspicious that the rotation had continued uninterrupted for 4.6 billion years without any oversight. Mr. Burrell said he did not. He said it was one of the most well-understood phenomena in physics.

Mr. Kwon asked Mr. Burrell whether he believed the committee's work had merit. Mr. Burrell paused for several seconds and then said he believed the committee could spend its time however it wished. Councilwoman Glover reminded members that questions to witnesses should pertain to the testimony.

Mr. Rael asked whether there was any point in the process, from the initial formation of the solar system to the present day, at which a person or persons had intervened to cause or manage the sunrise. Mr. Burrell said no.

Mrs. Mills asked whether it was possible that someone was responsible and that Mr. Burrell simply did not know about it. Mr. Burrell said he supposed anything was possible but that he had no reason to believe that was the case.

Councilwoman Glover thanked Mr. Burrell for his time. Mr. Burrell left the meeting.

**Discussion Item 3: Jurisdictional Questions**

Councilwoman Glover raised the question of whether the Town of Meridian had any legal authority to assign blame for the sunrise. She noted that the sunrise occurred outside town limits (the sun being approximately 93 million miles away) and that municipal authority generally did not extend to celestial bodies.

Mr. Rael suggested that the committee's authority derived not from the physical location of the sunrise but from its effects. The sunrise affected Meridian residents. It woke people up. It illuminated the town. These were impacts within the committee's jurisdiction.

Mrs. Coale added that the town had previously exercised authority over things it did not own — the committee had only to look at noise ordinances, which regulated sounds produced by entities outside town limits if those sounds were audible within town limits. The sunrise, she argued, was visible within town limits. If Meridian could regulate noise from the neighboring county, it could at least investigate light from the sun.

Mr. Kwon said he believed this analogy was flawed and that the committee was overstepping. Councilwoman Glover noted his objection for the record.

Mr. Kwon then asked whether the committee could legally compel anyone to do anything about the sunrise even if it did assign blame. Could they fine the sun? Could they issue a cease-and-desist? Councilwoman Glover said these were questions for the Town Attorney and directed the secretary to invite the Town Attorney to the next meeting.

**Discussion Item 4: Definition of Blame**

Mrs. Mills raised a procedural question: the committee had been tasked with assigning blame, but had not yet defined what constituted blame. She suggested the committee needed working definitions.

Mr. Rael proposed the following: "Blame, for the purposes of this committee, shall mean responsibility for a condition or event that affects the residents of Meridian without their explicit consent or prior authorization."

Mrs. Coale suggested adding: "Blame may be assigned to any person, entity, organization, natural or supernatural force, or process, whether animate or inanimate, whether currently existing or historical."

Mr. Kwon asked whether they were truly proposing to assign blame to a process. Mrs. Mills said she did not see why not.

Councilwoman Glover tabled the definition discussion until Meeting 3 to allow members time to consider the language.

**Adjournment:**

Mr. Rael moved to adjourn. Motion seconded by Mrs. Coale. Meeting adjourned at 9:47 PM.

**Next Meeting:** September 23rd, 7:00 PM, Room 4C.

# MEETING 3

**Date:** September 23rd
**Time Called to Order:** 7:00 PM
**Presiding:** Councilwoman Diane Glover, Chair
**Recording Secretary:** Marcus Fell, Town Clerk's Office
**Members Present:** Diane Glover, Robert Kwon, Sandra Mills, Patricia Coale, James Hoadley, Thomas Rael
**Members Absent:** None
**Quorum:** Established (6 of 6)
**Also Present:** Howard Peel (public), Janet Peel (public)

**Approval of Previous Minutes:**

Mrs. Coale moved to approve the minutes of Meeting 2 as distributed. Motion seconded by Mr. Hoadley. Minutes approved 6-0.

**Public Comment:**

Mr. Peel spoke again. He said he had brought his wife this time so she could see what her tax dollars were funding. Mrs. Peel did not speak. Councilwoman Glover thanked them both for attending.

**Guest Testimony: Angela Ferreira, Town Attorney**

Ms. Ferreira was sworn in. Councilwoman Glover asked whether the Town of Meridian had legal authority to investigate, regulate, or assign blame for the sunrise.

Ms. Ferreira said she had reviewed the relevant statutes and that, in her professional opinion, the town did not have this authority. The sunrise was not a municipal matter. The committee's findings would have no legal force. Any blame assigned would be purely symbolic.

Mrs. Mills asked whether symbolic blame had value. Ms. Ferreira said that was not a legal question.

Mr. Hoadley asked whether the town could issue a proclamation of blame, similar to the proclamations it issued for Arbor Day or for recognizing local business owners. Ms. Ferreira said the town could issue proclamations on virtually any topic. Whether they should was a matter for the committee and the Council.

Mr. Rael asked whether a proclamation of blame would appear in the official town record. Ms. Ferreira said it would.

Mr. Kwon stated for the record that he believed the committee was an embarrassment to the town and that the legal opinion confirmed it had no purpose. Councilwoman Glover noted his statement for the record.

Ms. Ferreira was thanked and excused.

**Discussion Item 4 (Resumed): Definition of Blame**

Councilwoman Glover returned to the tabled discussion. Mr. Rael's proposed definition was read into the record: "Blame, for the purposes of this committee, shall mean responsibility for a condition or event that affects the residents of Meridian without their explicit consent or prior authorization."

Mrs. Coale's proposed expansion was also read: "Blame may be assigned to any person, entity, organization, natural or supernatural force, or process, whether animate or inanimate, whether currently existing or historical."

Mr. Hoadley asked whether "supernatural force" was appropriate for a municipal document. Mrs. Coale said the committee would be remiss if it excluded any category of potential blame-holder without investigation.

Mr. Kwon moved to amend the definition to read: "Blame, for the purposes of this committee, shall be understood as a purely symbolic designation with no legal force or practical consequence, assigned for the purpose of providing emotional satisfaction to committee members." Motion died for lack of a second.

Mrs. Mills moved to adopt both the definition and the expansion as read. Motion seconded by Mr. Hoadley. Motion carried 5-1, with Mr. Kwon opposed.

**Discussion Item 5: Narrowing of Candidates**

Councilwoman Glover said the committee had now heard from a meteorologist and an attorney. The meteorologist had identified the sunrise as a natural, unmanaged process. The attorney had confirmed the committee's work was symbolic. She asked whether the committee was now prepared to begin identifying specific candidates for blame.

Mr. Rael said that if the sunrise was a natural process, as Mr. Burrell claimed, then the committee should consider whether natural processes could bear responsibility. If a person could be blamed for negligence, could a process be blamed for — what? Existing? Occurring?

Mrs. Mills said she had been thinking about this and had arrived at an uncomfortable possibility. What if the committee's job was not to blame the sunrise but to blame the fact that it continued? Not the event itself but the repetition. Someone — or something — was ensuring that it happened again and again. The regularity was the crime.

Mr. Hoadley said he had been watching the sunrise every morning since the committee was formed. He had been taking notes. He said the sunrise was beautiful. He said this with visible discomfort. He said the beauty bothered him more than the regularity. If it were merely regular — mechanical — he could accept it. But it was beautiful, and he did not know who to thank for that, and the not-knowing felt like a kind of theft.

Silence, approximately twelve seconds.

Councilwoman Glover said the committee would resume this discussion at the next meeting. She asked members to come prepared with specific candidates or categories of candidates for blame.

**Adjournment:**

Meeting adjourned at 10:31 PM on motion by Mrs. Mills, seconded by Mrs. Coale.

**Next Meeting:** September 30th, 7:00 PM, Room 4C.

**Date:** September 30th
**Time Called to Order:** 7:00 PM
**Presiding:** Councilwoman Diane Glover, Chair
**Recording Secretary:** Marcus Fell, Town Clerk's Office
**Members Present:** Diane Glover, Robert Kwon, Sandra Mills, Patricia Coale, James Hoadley, Thomas Rael

**Approval of Previous Minutes:**

Mr. Kwon noted that the minutes of Meeting 3 recorded a pause of "approximately twelve seconds" and asked whether the length of pauses was typically recorded. Councilwoman Glover said the secretary had included it at her direction because the silence had felt significant. Mr. Kwon said he did not understand what made a silence significant. **Public Comment:**

One of the unidentified individuals, who identified herself as Louise Barker, 14 Chestnut Avenue, said she had been reading the committee minutes on the town website. She said she wanted the committee to know that she found the sunrise painful. She said her husband had died on a morning in April and that every sunrise since felt like an insult — as though the world did not know or care that he was gone. She said she did not think anyone was responsible for the sunrise but that she appreciated the committee's willingness to act as though someone might be. She said it was the first time a municipal body had acknowledged that the sunrise was something that happened *to* people rather than merely something that occurred.

Mrs. Mills thanked Ms. Barker. Councilwoman Glover thanked Ms. Barker.

Mr. Peel said this was the most ridiculous thing he had ever witnessed and that the committee was validating grief by encouraging delusion. Councilwoman Glover thanked Mr. Peel for his comments.

**Discussion Item 6: Personal Statements — Why We Need Someone to Blame**

Councilwoman Glover said she wanted to deviate from the usual format. She said the conversation at the end of the last meeting had raised something she felt the committee needed to address directly. She said the committee had been investigating the sunrise as though it were a policy question — something to be managed. But she suspected that for each member, the question was personal. She said she would like to go around the table and give each member a chance to say, if they wished, why they needed someone to blame for the sunrise. She emphasized that this was voluntary.

She began with herself.

**Diane:**

"My daughter was born on a Tuesday. I remember the sunrise that morning because I had been awake all night. The labor was long. I was in a room with a window that faced east. At some point around five in the morning, the sky started to change, and I thought: this child is going to be born into light. And she was. The sun came up and she came out and the room was golden and I thought — who did this? Who arranged for this to happen in this particular way? Someone must have. Because it was too perfect to be random. I have been in public life for twenty-two years. I have never been able to shake the feeling that someone was responsible for that morning and that I ought to find them and thank them or — or hold them accountable. I am not sure which. I am not sure those are different things."

**Sandra:**

"I don't have a story like that. I've been thinking about this committee since we started and I think what bothers me is simpler. The sunrise happens whether I want it to or not. I wake up and it's light. I have no choice. I am forced into brightness every single day. I find it difficult to accept that there is no one to appeal to. No office, no department. It just happens. I am told it will keep happening. I suppose I want someone to blame for the fact that I have no one to blame."

**James:**

"I said last week that the beauty bothered me. I've been thinking about why. When my father was dying — it was four years ago, hospice care, the bed in the living room — he could see out the front window. On his last morning, the sunrise came through that window and it hit his face and he opened his eyes and he looked right at it and he said, 'Well, there it is.' And then he died. That was it. 'Well, there it is.' I have never known what he meant. Was he saying goodbye to it? Was he acknowledging it? Was he just describing what he saw? But I will tell you that the sunrise has been a question for me ever since. What is it? Why does it come? Who is making it happen? Because my father saw it and then he was gone and I need there to be a connection. I need someone to have been in charge of both of those things."

Silence, approximately twenty seconds.

**Patricia:**

"I think I joined this committee because I make decisions for a living. I am — I was — a project manager. I spent thirty years assigning responsibility. Every task had an owner. Every deadline had someone behind it. Every outcome had a cause that could be traced to a person or a team. I found this satisfying. It made the world legible. Since I retired, I have been looking for something to organize. When the Council established this committee, I thought: here is something. Here is an event that occurs every day with no assigned owner. I can fix that. I can make a list and assign names. I can bring order to it. But I'm beginning to think — and this is hard for me to say — that what I really want is for someone to have been in charge of my life. Not just the sunrise. All of it. I want someone to have been responsible for the way it has unfolded. Because if no one was, then it was just — random. Things happened. Things kept happening. No one was managing it. I find that intolerable. So I came here to assign blame to the sunrise because I cannot bear to admit that there is no one to assign for anything."

**Thomas:**

"I'll be brief. I am sixty-four years old. I have lived in this town my entire life. I have been married, divorced, married again. I have buried both my parents. I have watched my children grow up and move away. I have sat on three different municipal committees. And the sunrise has come up every single day of my life without anyone asking my permission. I am tired of things happening to me. I want one thing to happen because someone decided it should. Even if it's the sunrise. Even if it's the one thing that everyone agrees I cannot control. I want someone to have decided it. I want there to have been a decision."

**Robert:**

"I have opposed this committee from the beginning. You all know that. I think it is a waste of time. I think it is possibly a misuse of town resources. I think we look foolish. But I have come to every meeting because — because I think I am afraid of what you are doing. Not the committee itself but the thing underneath it. The need. I am afraid of the need. Because if you find someone to blame for the sunrise, then everything has a reason. Everything can be accounted for. And I don't want to live in that world. I want some things to just be. I want the sunrise to just be. But I keep coming to these meetings because I am not sure I am right. I am not sure the world I want is the real one. And I think I am afraid that it is. That it is just happening. All of it. That no one is in charge. That is what I have to say."

Diane did not call for a motion to close this agenda item. The committee proceeded to the next item without discussion of what had been shared.

**Discussion Item 7: Preliminary Candidate List**

Councilwoman Glover said that, in the spirit of the evening's discussion, she would like to propose the following candidates for blame:

1. The sun itself.
2. The Earth (for rotating).
3. God, gods, or any divine or supernatural creator.
4. The scientific processes described by Mr. Burrell in Meeting 2.
5. The concept of time.
6. The human perceptual system (for experiencing the sunrise as an event).
7. No one.

Mrs. Mills moved to accept the list as a working document. James seconded. Motion carried 6-0.

# MEETING 5

**Date:** October 7th

**Approval of Previous Minutes:**

Sandra noted that I had recorded the length of the silence following James's remarks at Meeting 4 as "approximately twenty seconds." She said it had been longer than that. She said it had felt like at least thirty seconds. James said he thought it had been about fifteen seconds. I said I had timed it at approximately twenty-two seconds and had recorded twenty to avoid seeming overly precise. Diane directed that the minutes reflect "approximately twenty seconds" as written.

**Public Comment:**

Howard Peel said he had read the minutes from last week's meeting and that he wished to formally apologize for his earlier characterization of the committee. He did not elaborate. He sat down.

Mrs. Peel placed her hand on Howard's arm after he sat down. I am recording this because it seemed significant.

**Discussion Item 8: Consideration of Candidates for Blame**

Diane opened discussion on the preliminary list.

**Candidate 1: The Sun Itself**

Thomas said he had difficulty assigning blame to an inanimate object. Sandra noted that the committee's definition of blame specifically included inanimate entities. James said that blaming the sun felt like blaming a tool rather than the person wielding it — the sun did not decide to rise, it simply existed.

Patricia said that if the sun was a tool, then someone must be wielding it, which only pushed the question back one level. Robert said this was the problem with the entire exercise: every answer led to another question.

No formal vote was taken on this candidate.

**Candidate 2: The Earth**

James said the Earth was not to blame for rotating because it had no choice in the matter. Sandra said this was also true of the sun. Thomas said that if the Earth had no choice, and the sun had no choice, and the process had no choice, then the committee was assigning blame to a chain of events in which no choice had been made, which seemed to hollow out the concept of blame entirely.

Diane said this was a fair observation.

**Candidate 3: God, Gods, or Divine/Supernatural Creator**

Sandra said she was not religious and did not feel comfortable voting on this. Thomas said he was religious and also did not feel comfortable voting on this. Patricia said the committee had a mandate to consider all candidates, including supernatural ones, and that discomfort was not a sufficient reason to exclude a candidate from consideration.

Robert said that assigning blame to God for the sunrise was theologically problematic because most religious traditions held that God was good and that the sunrise was good, so the committee would be assigning blame for something beneficial, which undermined the premise of blame.

Sandra said the committee had not established whether the sunrise was beneficial. She said it was certainly regular and beautiful, but that did not necessarily mean it was good. James asked what would make it good or bad and whether the committee was now in the business of evaluating the moral character of celestial phenomena.

Diane said this was a question worth considering but perhaps beyond the committee's scope.

**Candidate 4: Scientific Processes**

Thomas said this was what Mr. Burrell had described at Meeting 2. He said that assigning blame to scientific processes was essentially assigning blame to the way the universe worked, which was like assigning blame to gravity or to mathematics.

Sandra said she was not opposed to blaming mathematics. James said he thought blaming mathematics was where this was heading all along.

Sandra laughed. James also laughed. I believe this was the first instance of laughter in these proceedings.

**Candidate 5: The Concept of Time**

Patricia said that without time, there would be no succession of events, and therefore no sunrise as a discrete occurrence. The sunrise, she said, was a function of time as much as it was a function of planetary rotation.

Robert said that blaming time was like blaming existence itself and that the committee might as well blame the fact that there was something rather than nothing. Sandra said she was open to that.

**Candidate 6: The Human Perceptual System**

James said this candidate interested him. The sunrise was, in a sense, an experience. It did not exist as an event until someone perceived it — until light entered a human eye and was processed by a human brain and was given the name "sunrise." In that sense, the person experiencing the sunrise was participating in its creation. The viewer was not separate from the event.

Thomas said this was a philosophical position with significant implications. If the viewer was to blame for the sunrise, then each of them was to blame for every sunrise they had witnessed. Sandra said she found this troubling. Patricia said she found it clarifying.

Robert said that if they were all to blame, then the committee's work was done, because blame had been assigned. Diane said she was not sure collective self-blame was what the enabling resolution had in mind.

**Candidate 7: No One**

Diane said this was the candidate Robert had been advocating from the beginning. She asked Robert if he wished to speak to it.

Robert said he did. He said that "no one" was not a candidate — it was the absence of a candidate. It was the truth, as best he understood it. No one was to blame for the sunrise. It happened. It would keep happening. The committee could list candidates and vote and issue proclamations and none of it would change the fact that no one was in charge.

He said he understood why the other members needed someone to be responsible. He said he even felt the same need, sometimes. But he believed that the most honest thing the committee could do was to state clearly that the sunrise was uncaused by any agent, that it was a feature of existence, and that the question of blame, while meaningful, had no answer.

Sandra said that was the most depressing thing she had ever heard. Robert said he understood why she felt that way.

Silence. (I did not time this one. I am not sure why.)

**Vote on Candidates:**

Diane said the committee would not vote to assign blame this evening. She said the discussion had raised important questions and that she wanted members to have more time. She proposed that the committee vote at Meeting 6.

Thomas moved to table the vote. Patricia seconded. Motion carried 6-0.

**Additional Discussion: The Purpose of the Committee**

Sandra said she wanted to say something that was not on the agenda. She said she had been thinking about why the committee existed and she had come to believe that the question the committee was asking — who is responsible for the sunrise? — was more important than any answer they might arrive at. The question was the committee's purpose. Not the answer. The question.

James said he agreed. He said the act of sitting in a room together and asking "why?" about something that everyone took for granted was — he searched for the word. Sacred, he said. Then said he regretted using that word. Then said no, he meant it.

Thomas said he wanted to know what would happen to the committee after the vote. Diane said that would depend on what the Town Council decided. Thomas said he meant what would happen to *them* — the members. What would happen to the six of them after they stopped meeting about this. No one responded.

**Adjournment:**

Meeting adjourned at 11:17 PM on motion by James, seconded by Robert.

**Next Meeting:** October 14th, 7:00 PM, Room 4C.

She said she now looked at the sunrise and instead of feeling that it was an insult, she felt that it was a question — and that the question was enough.

Howard said nothing. He and Janet sat in the back row. They held hands. I am recording this at Sandra's request.

**Discussion Item 9: Final Deliberation on Assignment of Blame**

Diane said the evening's agenda contained a single item: the vote on assignment of blame. She said she wanted to give each member an opportunity to make a final statement before the vote.

**Thomas:**

"I have been thinking about something Sandra said last week. That the question is the purpose. I have been sitting with that. I don't need to assign blame anymore. I don't know when that changed. But I came into this wanting someone to have decided things, and I am leaving it — or I am beginning to leave it — with the sense that the deciding is what matters. Not the decider. The fact that we sat here and decided to ask. That was the thing."

**Patricia:**

"I still want to assign blame. I want it because I am who I am. I assign things. That is what I do. But I have realized that the assigning is the thing I need — not the assignment. I need the act of pointing at something and saying 'you.' That is what this committee has given me. A place to point."

**James:**

"My father said 'Well, there it is.' I have been thinking about those words. Maybe he was not describing the sunrise. Maybe he was describing everything. There it is. This is it. This is what there is. Maybe he was not greeting the sun. Maybe he was greeting existence. I don't need to know who sent it. I only need to know that it arrived."

**Sandra:**

"I want to thank this room. Not the people in it — though I want to thank them too — but the room. Room 4C. This fluorescent-lit rectangle in a municipal building where six people gathered to ask a question that had no answer. I think this is the most sacred space I have ever been in and I include in that every church I have ever entered. I don't know what we're voting on tonight and I don't think it matters."

**Robert:**

"I have nothing to add to what I've already said. No one is to blame. But I am grateful to have been here. I am grateful that the question was asked even though it has no answer. I did not expect to be grateful. I expected to be frustrated. I was frustrated. And then I was not."

**Diane:**

"My daughter is nine now. She does not know about this committee. She does not know that her mother has spent seven weeks trying to find out who was responsible for the morning she was born. I will tell her someday. I do not know what I will say. I think I will tell her that the sunrise was not caused by anyone. And that this was the most beautiful thing about it. And that it took me seven weeks and five other people to help me see that."

**The Vote:**

Diane said the committee would now vote. The question before the committee: should the committee assign blame for the sunrise to any candidate or candidates on the preliminary list, or should the committee decline to assign blame.

Sandra moved that the committee formally decline to assign blame for the sunrise. Robert seconded.

Motion carried 6-0.

Thomas exhaled. Sandra placed her pen down on the table. James looked at the window. It was dark outside. The sun had set during the meeting.

**Discussion Item 10: Recommendations to Town Council**

Diane said the committee should formulate a recommendation to the Town Council regarding the sunrise.

James moved that the committee recommend the following: "The Town of Meridian acknowledges that the sunrise occurs daily without known cause, agent, or authorization, and that this is acceptable."

Patricia seconded.

Motion carried 6-0.

**Discussion Item 11: Future of the Committee**

Diane noted that the committee's work was, by its own assessment, complete. The question had been asked and a determination had been made. She said she would recommend to the Town Council that the committee be dissolved.

Thomas asked whether the committee could meet one more time. Diane said the bylaws required a final meeting to approve the minutes and formally close the committee's records. She said that meeting would be next Monday.

Sandra asked whether the committee could just — meet. Not for any purpose. Just to be in the room together one more time. Diane said she did not see why not.

**Adjournment:**

Meeting adjourned at 8:44 PM on motion by Sandra, seconded by all present.

**Next Meeting:** October 21st, 7:00 PM, Room 4C (Final Meeting).
