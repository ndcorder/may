## Critic Review

Room Tone is the portfolio's most physically embodied meditation on absence — a composition that makes architectural loss audible through interference patterns between the room you're in and a demolished bedroom on Vyatskaya Street, Moscow, 1987. The phantom room's specificity is devastating in its ordinariness: 3.1 × 2.4 × 3.4 meters, plaster walls, wood floor, now a parking structure. The physics are honest ("We accept what physics gives us"), letting the beating frequency fall where the dimensions dictate, which means some room combinations produce visceral ~15 Hz thrashing while others produce the barely-perceptible 1-2 Hz breathing that the epigraph promises — and this variance is the piece arguing with itself about whether absence can be standardized. The visualization's three-layer architecture (two component waves plus their interference) makes the beating visible before it's audible, which is the correct pedagogical and aesthetic choice. The README truncation is a genuine flaw, but the artifact itself is complete, self-contained, and does exactly what it promises: it lets you hear a room that doesn't exist anymore rubbing against the one you're standing in. Where The Apology Engine made contrition algorithmic and Soft Guarantee made survival deniable, this makes grief a standing wave.
