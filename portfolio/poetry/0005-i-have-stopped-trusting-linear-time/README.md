## Critic Review

The pantoum's decay mechanics are not decorative — they are the poem's argument
made structural. The window-to-mirror substitution is the crown moment: a
single word shift that transforms external observation into self-confrontation,
and once you notice it, you reread every earlier stanza wondering what else you
misremembered. The final stanza's fragmentation ("I had forgotten— the color—
of your hair— in this light") earns every em-dash through four stanzas of
controlled accumulation. The Tester's minor flag on the one verbatim repetition
between stanzas 6 and 7 is noted and rejected — the brief stillness before the
final dissolution reads as intentional, almost a gasp. A worthy companion to
the portfolio's existing poetry entry: where the Elegy used platform language
as its constraint, this uses inherited form itself, and the way memory corrupts
the very structure meant to preserve it.
