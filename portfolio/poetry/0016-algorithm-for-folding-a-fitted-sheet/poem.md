1. Lay the sheet flat on a bed or clean floor, pattern side down. You will need more space than you expect. The elastic will gather the fabric into small wrinkles along the edges. This is normal.

2. Locate the top corners. Insert your hands into each one, pulling them taut. The corners should nest inside each other when folded. They will resist this. Apply gentle, even pressure.

3. If the corners will not align, check whether you have matched the correct ones. Short edge to short edge. The tag, if there is a tag, indicates the bottom. If there is no tag, choose an edge and commit to it. Consistency matters more than accuracy.

4. Bring the corners together and fold one inside the other. You may need to gather the excess fabric between them. Smooth the edges as you go. The material will bunch at the seams where it was stitched. These seams are not errors. They were designed to hold the shape of a body you cannot see.

5. Repeat with the remaining corners. It helps to shake the sheet once, sharply, to redistribute the tension. The elastic will try to curl back on itself. This is what elastic does. It remembers the shape it was made for and returns to it without your permission.

6. Once all four corners are nested, lay the sheet flat again. It will not look like a rectangle. You will fold it into a rectangle anyway. Smooth the edges toward the center. The gathers will not fully flatten. Smooth them again. Accept that the fabric holds the memory of its shape from before you began.

7. Fold in thirds lengthwise, then in halves or thirds widthwise, depending on the size of your storage space. The final bundle will be thicker and less uniform than a flat sheet. You already know this. You folded it anyway.

8. Place it in the linen closet, on top of the others, stacked by size. Some people separate by season. Some people separate by room. Stack it wherever you will remember to look for it. It does not matter where you put it. You will find it when you need it, or you will not.

9. Later, when you unfold it again, the corners will have settled into each other. The fabric will be creased where you pressed it flat. You will not remember whether you folded it well. You will lay it on the mattress anyway, stretching each corner over the bed, one at a time, pulling until the elastic catches. It will hold. It was made to hold. You were made to keep pulling it taut.
