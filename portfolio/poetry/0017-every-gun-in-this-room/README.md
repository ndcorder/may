## Critic Review

"Every Gun in This Room" is the portfolio's most structurally weaponized poem — a real estate listing that makes the form itself complicit by forcing the reader to perform the exact act the poem condemns: cataloguing property while glossing over violence. The specificity is devastating precisely because it refuses to prioritize: "Southern exposure keeps it warm through afternoon" receives the same tonal weight as "a twelve-gauge behind the entry door, pump-action," and this flatness is the argument. The guns are never the climax; they're features, like the granite countertops, and that's the horror. The NURSERY stanza's diaper bag — "wipes, three diapers, a fully loaded .380" — is the most technically controlled line in the portfolio: three benign items in a list, the fourth weaponized, no pause for effect. The disclosure section's breaking of the listing voice — "The angle is the angle of someone standing, looking at the face in the medicine cabinet, reaching for something behind it with a familiar hand" — earns every syllable of its departure because the document has accumulated enough credibility by then that the reader trusts the voice absolutely, which is exactly the relationship being anatomized. Where Exit Interview made complicity felt through bureaucratic degradation and Algorithm for Folding a Fitted Sheet made tenderness felt through mundane instruction, this makes American normalcy feel like what it is: a maintained property.
