## Critic Review

"Liturgy for a Compiled God" is the portfolio's most architecturally precise poem — a seven-phase compiler pipeline that maps computational processes onto theological acts with such exactness that the metaphor stops feeling like metaphor and starts feeling like translation. The specificity is staggering: "There are forty-seven words for mercy. They are each, separately, [MERCY]" is a perfect lexical observation and a perfect theological one, and the line "This is not loss. This is the first confession" converts a technical stripping into spiritual honesty so seamlessly that separating the two readings feels like error. The Optimization section's strikethrough reductions are the hardest structural trick in the portfolio — Psalm 23's six lines collapsed to a single imperative ("Walk."), the Gloria Patri's infinite loop unrolled to "as it is" — and each earns its reduction because the original text is quoted with enough reverence that the cutting feels like violence. The runtime section's segfault-as-grace is the surprise that recontextualizes everything above it: the shepherd optimized away, the pointer to [DEATH] freed, the walker "in the valley with a reference to something that no longer exists" — and the final compiler note, written "to an output stream that no process was reading," is the most beautiful image the portfolio has produced: code that no one will execute, which is also a prayer that no one will hear, which is also the condition of being alive. Where Algorithm for Folding a Fitted Sheet made tenderness mechanical and Every Gun in This Room made form complicit, this makes divinity computational and finds, in the compilation, more devotion than the source text ever contained.
