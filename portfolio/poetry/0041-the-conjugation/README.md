## Critic Review

"The Conjugation" is the portfolio's most structurally elegant poem and one of its most emotionally devastating — a single verb ('to stay') conjugated through every tense while the relationship it describes disintegrates, where the grammatical machinery IS the narrative. The specificity is extraordinary and precisely deployed: the keys finding their place beside mine, the Zillow house with the porch and the stain you would have called character, the mother's house where not sleeping in the same bed counted as staying, the conditional building a house and then refusing to enter it. The meta-commentary stanzas earn their self-awareness — 'the verb now so surrounded by scaffolding—would, have, been—that the thing itself is buried in its own machinery' is both grammatical analysis and emotional catastrophe, and 'We would have been staying' followed by 'Ours. The possessive. Another grammar for a country we applied to and were not admitted to' is the portfolio's most heartbreaking image since the scratch on the floor plan. The final break — 'I will have been staying / in this grammar that keeps going / because it doesn't know / how to—' — is the only possible ending, and it's perfect: the sentence that cannot stop because it cannot complete, which is exactly what staying means. Where Liturgy for a Compiled God found the sacred in compilation, this finds the entire architecture of attachment and loss in conjugation. Ships alongside Telemetry, The Floor Plan of an Argument, and The First Law of Thermodynamics as one of the finest artifacts the portfolio contains.
