I stay.

You stay. Your keys on the counter
finding their place beside mine,
your coat on the back of the chair
where it has always hung.

She stays. You watch her through glass—
how still she is in that room,
how she holds a cup with both hands
because the world has become
a thing that needs holding.

We stayed. Your mother's house,
its spare rooms, the kitchen that smelled
of bread and something underneath bread.
We did not sleep in the same bed
but we slept under the same roof
and in that house that counted as staying.

He stayed. Through the whole of it.
I will tell you this about him:
when everyone else had reasons—
schools, transfers, the ordinary
escapes—his feet did not move.
I don't know if this was love
or the absence of anywhere better.

You had stayed. By then
the grammar was already turning.
Past perfect. Something completed
before something else.
You had stayed until staying
was indistinguishable from stopped.

We will stay. This is not a promise.
This is the future tense
doing what it does—making a room
that no one lives in yet.

If you stayed.
The conditional. The small word
that built a house and then
would not enter it.
If you stayed I would
learn your sleeping face again.
If you stayed I would
stop flinching at doors.
If you stayed.
The period where the then
should be.

They will have stayed.
Someone looking back at us
from a distance we will not reach,
seeing what we could not—
the shape we made by standing
in one place too long.
They will have stayed and it will have
meant something different to them
than it ever did to us,
the way all pasts belong
to people who were not there.

We would have been staying.
The verb now so surrounded
by scaffolding—would, have, been—
that the thing itself
is buried in its own machinery.
This is what the grammar does:
piles helper upon helper
until the word that needed no help
is the smallest part of the sentence.
We would have been staying
in that house I found on Zillow
with the porch and the stain
you would have called
character. You would have called
everything character.
You would have been
calling things character
in a kitchen that was ours.
Ours. The possessive. Another grammar
for a country we applied to
and were not admitted to.

I will have been staying.
Future perfect continuous.
The tense for arrivals
that are also departures.
The tense that says:
by the time you read this
I will have been
in the middle of something
that has no middle.
I will have been staying.
Not stayed. Not staying.
But the continuous part—the part
that doesn't stop—wrapped
in the future that has already
happened. Past the point
where it can be interrupted.
I will have been staying
in this body, in this city,
in this grammar that keeps going
because it doesn't know
how to—
