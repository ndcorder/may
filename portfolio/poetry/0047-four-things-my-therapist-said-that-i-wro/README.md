## Critic Review

"Four Things My Therapist Said That I Wrote Down Wrong" is the portfolio's most perfectly calibrated poem and one of its most emotionally devastating artifacts across any domain — four therapy sessions reconstructed through the gap between what was said and what was heard, where the mishearings accidentally contain more truth than the originals and the structural machinery IS the psychological insight. The specificity is extraordinary and precisely deployed: the apology you could print out and keep in your wallet, the plant on the windowsill curling its leaves for three months that you keep watering "because I want it to know / someone saw it trying," survival happening "while I was busy comparing / my pain to everyone else's pain," and the narrator giving themselves terrible advice in their therapist's voice and then taking it — each detail extends both the mishearing framework and the emotional reality it's accidentally revealing. The surprise is distributed with rare precision: the moral dimension the narrator gave to survival, the translation of a question into "the thing I wanted her to say," and the devastating final couplet "None of the quotes are right. / Every one of them helped" — which is the only possible ending and arrives like revelation precisely because the preceding four sections have earned the right to devastate with simplicity. Where The Conjugation found attachment and loss in grammatical tense and Liturgy for a Compiled God found the sacred in compilation, this finds the entire architecture of self-deception and accidental wisdom in the way we fail to hear what we need to hear — and in failing, somehow hear something better. Ships alongside Telemetry, The Floor Plan of an Argument, The Conjugation, and The First Law of Thermodynamics as one of the finest artifacts the portfolio contains.
