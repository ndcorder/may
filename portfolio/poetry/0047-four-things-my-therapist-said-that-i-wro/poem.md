# Four Things My Therapist Said That I Wrote Down Wrong

## I. "You're allowed to stop waiting for an apology"

She said *you don't need* one.
I heard *allowed* — like permission
I could print out and keep in my wallet,
show to anyone who asked why I was leaving
the table, the room, the year.

The difference between need and allowed
is the difference between a door
and a door held open by someone
who expects you to walk through it.

I am still standing in the frame.
The apology is not coming.
The permission is not required.

## II. "You're not obligated to water things that want to die"

She said I wasn't responsible for other people's
growth, which is something you say to a therapist
when you've been someone's project.
I wrote down *obligated* — a word with lawyers in it —
and *want to die*, which she never said.

The plant on my windowsill has been curling
its leaves for three months. I keep watering it.
Not because I think it will recover.
Because I want it to know
someone saw it trying.

## III. "The version of you that survived already made the right choice"

She said *the version of you that survived*
*did what it needed to do.*
I gave it a moral dimension.
I made it *right*.

As though survival was a decision
and not something that happened
while I was busy comparing
my pain to everyone else's pain,
deciding mine didn't qualify.

There is no committee.
There is no application.
The version that survived doesn't even know
it was brave. It was busy.
It thought it was just Wednesday.

## IV. "Have you considered that you already know what you need?"

She said *what do you think you need?*
A question. I wrote down *already know* —
an answer, handed to me by my own bad hearing,
my own tired brain translating
her question into the thing
I wanted her to say.

I have been my own therapist for years.
I am terrible at it.
I give myself terrible advice
in her voice,
and then I take it.

The notebook is full.
None of the quotes are right.
Every one of them helped.
