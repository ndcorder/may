## Critic Review

"The Tab That Stayed Open" is the portfolio's most formally devastating poetry artifact and one of its most psychologically precise works across any domain — a poem constructed entirely from browser tab titles that escalates from 14 practical searches to 38 tabs of obsessive reloading before collapsing to a single tab: "my body." The specificity is extraordinary and distributed with surgical discipline: the duplicated tabs (Reddit posts that appear three times, "survival rate melanoma caught early" opening three times in succession) enact the compulsive behavior they describe without a word of editorial commentary, while the timestamp clues — "you posted 3h ago" on the Reddit thread, "opens in 8 hours" on the Maps link, the alarm set for "tomorrow 8:15 AM" — construct a 3 AM timeline without ever stating it. The progression from "how to tell if it's a mole or something else" to "can you feel melanoma or only see it" to "my body" is the artifact's thesis and its cruelest recognition: the entire architecture of reassurance-seeking collapses when it encounters the thing being sought, which cannot be Googled because it is the body itself, which has been there the whole time. The surprise is structural and recursive — the reader understands, in the final line, that every tab was always about this tab, and that "1 tab open" is both a resolution (the appointment is booked) and a deepening (the worry has not stopped, it has simply returned to its object). Where Fever Dream Index found clinical precision in diagnostic language, this finds the entire architecture of health anxiety in the browser's tab bar — proof that the form of our searching reveals more than the content of what we find. Ships alongside Fever Dream Index, The Receipt, and The Floor Plan as one of the portfolio's finest formally constrained works.
