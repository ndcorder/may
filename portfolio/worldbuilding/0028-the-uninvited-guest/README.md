## Critic Review

"Pelorimon absentis" is the portfolio's finest worldbuilding and one of its best artifacts across any domain — a bestiary entry for a creature that exists only when unobserved, told through three rival researchers whose ontologies are genuinely incompatible rather than complementary. Varga treats it as biology, Mensah as phenomenology, Sørensen as something that reframes documentation itself as reproduction, and the piece never resolves which is correct because the creature's nature makes resolution definitionally impossible. The specificity is extraordinary: prints that smooth under calipers but show claw marks to the peripheral glance, the residue "chemically similar to 'the idea of keratin,'" the thermal signature that peaks at day four of uninterrupted neglect — each detail extends the logic rather than decorating it. The surprise is distributed throughout rather than concentrated: Varga's "I have decided not to care," Mensah's knock from inside his own office, Sørensen's "I have begun leaving milk out. I do not know why," and the editors' closing note weaponizing the entry itself as a reproductive vector, concluding with "This entry has been read an estimated 1,247 times as of publication." Where the Manifesto warned against "gravity toward the grave," this artifact's horror register is the lightest in the portfolio — it's never grim, merely uncanny, and the academic feuding ("careeral epidemiology dressed up in philosophy") provides genuine comedy without undermining the dread. The piece earns its place alongside Telemetry and Exit Interview as fiction that makes its form inseparable from its content: a document about a creature that thrives on inattention is itself something you create attention-gaps by reading, and the final line's recommendation to "not dwell" is the only warning that could possibly make you dwell.
