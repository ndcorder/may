# Pelorimon absentis
## (The Uninvited Guest)

**Kingdom:** Animalia (disputed)  
**Phylum:** Unclassified (see Mensah, 2019)  
**Habitat:** Attention gaps, peripheral spaces, unobserved rooms  
**Threat Level:** Indeterminate (see Addendum C)

---

## I. Description

*Pelorimon absentis* is a medium-sized organism (est. 40–80 cm at shoulder, though measurements are methodologically compromised—see §IV) that manifests exclusively during periods of cognitive disattention. No confirmed visual representation exists. Attempts at photography produce images of the space the subject previously occupied; attempts at sketching from memory produce drawings that witnesses describe as "almost right but fundamentally wrong, like a description of a description" (Varga, 2021).

Track analysis suggests four limbs, though the gait pattern is inconsistent—varying between plantigrade and digitigrade within single trackways (Varga & Tanaka, 2020). Hair or fur samples have been collected but disintegrate under spectroscopic analysis, leaving behind a residue chemically similar to "the idea of keratin" (Mensah, 2019, p. 44, emphasis in original).

The creature's most reliable trace is thermal: unobserved spaces it has recently occupied register 2–4°C above ambient, with the warmth persisting 15–40 minutes after the subject's departure (or materialization; the distinction is ontologically loaded—see §III).

## II. Field Notes

### Dr. Emese Varga
*University of Budapest, Department of Cryptozoological Taxonomy*  
*Field season: Örség National Park, Hungary, 2019–2023*

**21 March 2020** — First confirmed trackway. Snow cover, predawn. Five prints in a diagonal gait crossing the abandoned forestry road near Szalafő. Prints deep, well-formed. I photographed them from multiple angles. The photographs show only snow. I sketched them instead. The sketches are accurate. I think. I can't look at them too long.

**14 October 2020** — Displaced objects in Research Station B. A coffee mug moved 30 cm overnight. The motion sensor logged nothing. I set up a ruler and camera. Next morning: the mug had moved again. The photograph shows the mug in its original position. The ruler reads 30 cm of displacement. The camera and the ruler disagree. I am choosing to trust the ruler.

**3 June 2021** — **Tracking breakthrough.** I have discovered that the prints improve in detail the less carefully I examine them. When I approach with calipers and a hand lens, the prints smooth into unmarked snow. When I walk past without stopping, glancing only peripherally, I can see individual claw marks, pad creases, even what appears to be a scar on the left hind foot. I have taken to walking past the track sites very quickly, pretending not to notice them, then writing down what I saw from the corner of my eye.

I realize this is not how field biology works. I have decided not to care.

**19 September 2022** — The creature has a preferred temperature gradient. It nests in spaces that go unthought-of for longest: the guest room we never use, the back of the supply closet, the space behind the research station where no one goes because there's nothing there. The warmth signature peaks at day 4 of uninterrupted neglect and stabilizes. It is building something. I don't know what.

### Dr. Kofi Mensah
*University of Cape Coast, Institute for Phenomenological Ecology*  
*Field season: Various locations, 2018–present*

Varga treats the subject as a biological organism. This is like treating a shadow as a plant. The creature—perhaps "event" is better—does not *have* an ecology. It *is* an ecology: the ecology of not-looking.

Consider: a phenomenon that manifests only when unobserved is, by definition, a relationship between observer and absence. Remove the observer, and you remove the condition of manifestation. *Pelorimon absentis* is not a thing that hides. It is the *hiding itself*—the shape that attention's withdrawal makes in the world.

**Field note, 12 August 2021** — I conducted an experiment in controlled inattention. Six volunteers sat in an empty room, instructed to think about anything except the center of the room. Thermal cameras registered a 3.2°C warming in the ignored space after eleven minutes. At minute twelve, one volunteer glanced at the center. Temperature dropped to ambient within four seconds. The volunteer reported "a feeling like something had been there, but I couldn't say what." I asked what she meant. She said, "Like the air was thicker, and then it wasn't."

**Field note, 7 January 2022** — There was a knock on my office door while I was writing. I wasn't expecting anyone. I called "Come in" but no one entered. The hallway was empty. My coffee was warm when it shouldn't have been. I returned to my notes. It wasn't until I was falling asleep that I realized: the knock came from inside the office.

I have no interpretation for this. I am recording it for completeness.

### Dr. Lina Sørensen
*University of Copenhagen, Department of Philosophy of Science (on leave)*  
*Field season: "Continuous" (personal correspondence, 2023)*

Both Varga and Mensah are partially correct and both are missing the crucial synthesis. The creature is neither organism nor epistemic event but something for which we lack a category—an *attention-dependent entity* that is as real as anything else while being real in a way nothing else is.

The difficulty is methodological. To study *P. absentis* is to think about it, and to think about it is to create the conditions of its absence. But this creates a paradox: the researcher who studies the creature most rigorously encounters it least, while the researcher who forgets about it encounters it most. What does it mean that the best method for studying this subject is *to stop studying it*?

I have begun leaving my research notes open on my desk when I leave the room. When I return, the warmth signature is present. Something has been reading. Or nesting. Or both.

I have begun leaving milk out. I do not know why.

## III. The Reproduction Question

The single most contentious issue in *P. absentis* research is whether the creature reproduces, and if so, how.

**Varga** proposes fission: when a nesting site becomes too established—when the attention-gap grows sufficiently large—the creature splits. She cites instances where two distinct thermal signatures appeared in adjacent neglected spaces (Varga, 2023). "It divides the way a cell divides," she writes, "but the mitosis is cognitive, not biological. Two people ignoring two different rooms is, to this creature, what nutrient abundance is to a bacterium."

**Mensah** rejects this. "The creature does not reproduce because it does not have a self to duplicate. What Varga observes is not fission but *refraction*: the same event viewed through two different gaps in attention, producing two shadows of the same absence" (Mensah, 2023, p. 112).

**Sørensen** proposes a third position: the creature reproduces through *documentation*. "Every paper we write about it, every field note, every conference presentation—these create new attention-gaps in readers and listeners. The person who skims past our paper on their way to something else has just created a nesting site in their own peripheral awareness. We are not describing the creature. We are *distributing* it" (Sørensen, 2024, fn. 14).

This latter claim has not been well-received. Varga called it "careeral epidemiology dressed up in philosophy" (peer review comment, *Journal of Speculative Biology*, 2024). Mensah's response was more measured but noted that "if Sørensen is correct, then her own paper is the most efficient act of reproduction the creature has ever accomplished" (ibid.).

## IV. Methodological Notes

Researchers are advised that direct observation of *P. absentis* is definitionally impossible. The following methods have proven productive:

1. **Peripheral documentation:** Glancing at evidence without focusing, then recording from memory.
2. **Thermal monitoring:** Reliable but uninformative regarding morphology.
3. **Neglect cultivation:** Establishing "attention-exclusion zones" and monitoring via automated equipment that is subsequently *not reviewed.* (Note: the data that is never looked at is the data most likely to contain evidence. This creates obvious archival problems.)
4. **Controlled forgetting:** Deliberately failing to check equipment, missing scheduled observations, or "accidentally" leaving recording devices running while distracted. Several researchers report that their best data comes from days they forgot they were conducting research.

## V. Addendum A — On the Question of Predation

There are no confirmed instances of *P. absentis* harming a human or animal. Varga classifies it as commensal. Mensah classifies it as neither—calling it "as harmless as the dark, and equally fundamental" (Mensah, 2020).

Sørensen's final field note (personal correspondence, February 2024) reads, in its entirety: "It doesn't eat. It doesn't need to. But I've noticed I've started forgetting things. Small things. Where I put my keys. What I came into a room for. Whether I already wrote this down. Little gaps. Little nests. I think it's comfortable here. I think I'm the habitat."

Dr. Sørensen has not responded to correspondence since March 2024. Her university reports that she is "on extended personal leave." Her office, according to a colleague who wished to remain anonymous, is "very warm."

---

## VI. Note on This Entry

The editors wish to inform readers that this bestiary entry, like all descriptions of *Pelorimon absentis*, constitutes an act of distribution as described by Sørensen (2024). By reading this far, you have created a small gap in your attention that was not there before. The gap is shaped like the description you have just read. Something may use it.

You are, of course, free to think about this. Thinking about it will close the gap. For now.

The editors recommend not dwelling.
